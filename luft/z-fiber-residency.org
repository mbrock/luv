#+title: Z-fiber residency and meshing

* Status

This is a design note, not an implemented representation.  It records a
promising resident-world layout suggested by the CPU mesher benchmark.

LUFT's chains remain immutable algebraic values.  This note proposes a
separate derived representation for a resident, editable block world and its
hot meshing path.  It follows the separation already stated in
[[file:luft-refoundation-brief.md][the refoundation brief]]: mutable occupancy
must not dictate the mathematical chain representation.

* Observation

LUFT has 255 valid vertical cells, from ~z=0~ through ~z=254~.  Expected block
worlds usually have simple vertical fibers: air above terrain, a few material
strata, and occasional floors, roofs, caves, or edited cells.  Those material
fibers should generally have few runs.

The vertical extent also fits in 256 occupancy bits.  This makes a complete
=(x,y)= fiber exactly four =u64= words, with bit 255 permanently zero.  The
extra bit is a useful sentinel for the open top of the world.

These two facts suggest parallel resident representations:

- ordered material runs for semantic storage;
- a 256-bit occupancy cache for hot local queries and Boolean meshing.

RLE is not proposed as the inner-loop occupancy query.  Even short run lists
would reintroduce searching where a fixed bit mask permits direct operations.

* Proposed resident column

A starting residency unit is a full-height horizontal column:

#+begin_example
16 x 16 x 255 resident cells
16 x 16 x 16 independently replaceable mesh sections
one fiber of X/Y halo around the resident column
#+end_example

A 16 by 16 column's occupancy cache occupies 8 KiB.  Including a one-fiber
X/Y halo, 18 by 18 fibers occupy about 10 KiB.  A 32 by 32 column would occupy
32 KiB before its halo and remains worth benchmarking as a coarser streaming
unit.

Residency and mesh publication need not have the same grain.  A full-height
column may be loaded and edited as one semantic unit while its sixteen
16-cell-high mesh sections are rebuilt and replaced independently.

An unloaded neighboring column is not air.  Residency state must distinguish
unavailable data from resident air.  Meshing may read halo fibers but emits
only faces owned by the central mesh section.

* Material fibers

A material fiber is an ordered sequence of non-overlapping half-open runs
=[z-min,z-max)= with a material value.  A resident fiber covers =[0,255)=;
air is an ordinary material value rather than an absent neighbor.

A single-cell edit splits at most one run into three.  Adjacent runs with the
same material coalesce immediately.  The exact physical encoding, maximum run
count, and fallback for noisy fibers remain measurements rather than design
assumptions.

The occupancy cache is updated with the same edit by setting or clearing one
bit.  Material runs own meaning; occupancy bits are a cheap derived product.

* Bit-fiber surface extraction

Let =S(x,y)= be the 256-bit solid mask for one fiber.  Horizontal exposed-face
masks are Boolean differences between neighboring fibers:

#+begin_example
+X faces = S(x,y) AND NOT S(x+1,y)
-X faces = S(x,y) AND NOT S(x-1,y)
+Y faces = S(x,y) AND NOT S(x,y+1)
-Y faces = S(x,y) AND NOT S(x,y-1)
#+end_example

Vertical exposed-face masks are =S= combined with a one-bit vertical shift of
=S=.  The implementation must explicitly carry bits across =u64= lanes and
treat bit 255 and cells below zero as air.

This route emits exposed faces directly.  It does not construct six boundary
sites per solid cell, comparison-sort them, and cancel internal pairs.

Set bits in each resulting mask identify the Z coordinates at which compact
face records must be emitted.  A scalar trailing-zero loop is a natural first
implementation.  Material lookup can walk the already ordered run list with a
cursor while emitted Z coordinates are ascending.

* Edge and corner classification

LUFT classifies the occupancy star around each exposed face's four edges and
four corners.  Neighboring fiber masks can express those star configurations
as Boolean combinations:

- four incident fibers determine an edge's configurations along Z;
- neighboring fibers and one-bit vertical shifts determine corner
  configurations;
- result masks identify every Z position with a particular classification.

The first implementation should preserve the existing scalar classifier as an
oracle.  A bit-fiber implementation must produce identical shape words and
face records for every tested occupancy pattern.

* Camera-connected air boundary

The complete solid/air boundary is not necessarily the boundary useful to a
camera above terrain.  It also contains the underside of the ground, sealed
caves, and every other air pocket.  A second experiment instead starts at the
top cell of the center fiber, finds that camera's connected air component,
and emits only solid faces incident to it.

There are three deliberately comparable ways to discover the component:

- breadth-first search over individual air cells, as the simple oracle;
- fixed-point propagation through the four-word occupancy masks;
- breadth-first search over maximal empty Z runs, where runs in adjacent
  fibers are connected exactly when their Z intervals overlap.

The last form is the vertical-ray idea in discrete form.  One empty interval
stands for a whole unobstructed segment of a Z ray; horizontal interval
intersection says where air can cross into the neighboring ray.  After the
reachable intervals are materialized as masks, Boolean neighbor operations
produce the six oriented camera-air face masks.

The benchmark keeps a one-fiber X/Y halo and does not treat missing residency
as air.  Its interval index is built with the benchmark case and retained, so
the timed =AIR-RUNS= phase measures traversal of a resident run representation,
not construction of that representation.  The cell path constructs the face
masks cell by cell and is the exact oracle for both reachability and packed
boundary shifts.

* SBCL SIMD shape

Four =u64= words are one 256-bit fiber, making AVX2-width execution a natural
experiment with =sb-simd=.  SIMD is most promising for homogeneous Boolean
work:

- horizontal exposed-face masks;
- vertical shifts and exposed-face masks;
- edge and corner occupancy-pattern masks;
- batches of adjacent fibers when wider vectors are available.

SIMD is not automatically useful for RLE edits, variable-length material
runs, set-bit enumeration, or variable-count record emission.  Those should
remain scalar unless profiles say otherwise.

Keep a straightforward scalar bit-fiber kernel.  Select a supported SIMD
kernel outside the hot loop, compare its masks and records differentially, and
inspect generated code before claiming a vector speedup.  Array layout and
alignment must be measured with the actual =sb-simd= representation.

* Current evidence

The August 2026 deterministic CPU benchmark currently shows, for a solid
64-cubed case:

- complete chain-based materialization: about 960 ms;
- surface-chain construction: about 800 ms;
- chain-backed face classification: about 159 ms;
- dense-window face classification: about 62 ms.

Statistical profiling attributes about 75 percent of surface construction to
sorting the boundary candidates.  It attributes about 73 percent of
chain-backed face classification beneath chain occupancy lookup, including
about 51 percent in the binary-search lower bound.

Adding precise array and integer declarations to an isolated lower-bound
experiment reduced chain-backed classification from about 159 ms to 101--102
ms with bit-identical records.  It did not improve the roughly 800 ms surface
construction: the general comparison sort remained dominant.

Hoisting the per-cell boundary callback produced an identical surface chain
and reduced allocation from about 32.25 MiB to 24.23 MiB, but did not change
the roughly 793 ms runtime.  This separates a useful allocation cleanup from
the larger algorithmic target.

The executable =luft/z-fiber-benchmark.lisp= now measures the first direct
bit-fiber implementation.  On the August 2026 x86-64 development machine, a
16 by 16 terrain case takes approximately:

- 0.94 ms for camera-air cell traversal and cell-by-cell boundary output;
- 1.63 ms for whole-field one-cell-at-a-time bit-mask propagation;
- 0.015 ms for traversal of 324 pre-indexed halo air runs and packed boundary
  output.

The cave case has 470 halo air runs and takes approximately 0.015 ms by the
same interval path, versus 0.94 ms by the cell oracle.  These are traversal
measurements rather than end-to-end residency construction measurements.
They make the interval graph promising while also showing that naive global
bitset fixed-point propagation is slower than the ordinary cell queue.

* Evidence gates

Before adopting the representation, build a scalar reference implementation
and compare it with the existing chain path.  Require exact agreement for:

- exposed oriented faces;
- edge and corner shape words;
- material/stock assignment;
- faces crossing mesh-section and resident-column boundaries;
- edits at ~z=0~ and ~z=254~;
- unavailable neighbor handling;
- solid, terrain, architecture, checkerboard, cave, and randomized corpora.

Benchmark at least 8-, 16-, and 32-cell mesh-section widths, and 16- and
32-cell resident-column widths.  Record wall time, allocation, occupied cells,
material runs, emitted faces, dirty sections, halo crossings, and output
bytes.  Measure scalar bit fibers before SIMD so vector speedup has an honest
reference.

The representation earns adoption if it removes surface sorting, keeps edit
and publication granularity small, and improves end-to-end remeshing without
making pathological material fibers or boundary semantics obscure.

* Open questions

- Should residency begin at 16 by 16 full-height columns, or should streaming
  group several such columns under one owner?
- Should material runs cover air explicitly or use a separate canonical air
  convention at the semantic boundary?
- How are transparent and non-solid materials represented in occupancy and
  face ownership?
- Can face records be emitted directly in the renderer's required polarity
  order without a second sort?
- Which edge and corner rules admit clear bit-sliced kernels, and which remain
  clearer as scalar work over emitted faces?
- When does a noisy fiber switch from runs to a dense material encoding?
- How are derived occupancy, mesh-section revisions, and asynchronous
  publication stamped so stale work cannot replace a newer edit?

This representation gives you a much stronger way to pursue the “ZSnes
reconstruction alphabet, but for voxel topology” idea.

The eight occupied-or-empty cells around a lattice vertex form an
8-bit signature. There are 256 raw signatures, but cube rotations,
reflections, and solid/air complementation reduce them to a relatively
small family of canonical cases—the same sort of symmetry reduction
that makes marching-cubes case tables manageable.

You could have a local reconstruction alphabet whose entries answer:

Is this a convex corner, a concave corner, a diagonal contact, a
tunnel-like saddle, or a flat continuation?  Where should the shared
vertex move?  What edge strips and corner caps should exist?  What
normal or normal cone should each feature have?  Should diagonally
touching solids remain separated or receive a bridge?  How rounded
should the feature become at this level of detail?

The important difference from ordinary marching cubes is that you do
not have to abandon the cubical world or pretend it was sampled from
an unknown smooth scalar field. The cellular complex is
authoritative. The case table is merely choosing a geometric
realization of known cell incidence.

A particularly clean mesh-shader decomposition would be site-centric:

each exposed face emits its central face patch; each relevant edge
site emits one bevel or fillet strip; each relevant vertex site emits
one corner cap.

Because each edge and vertex is globally canonical, every feature has
exactly one owner without being “owned by a voxel.” That should reduce
duplicate computation and make increasingly elaborate fillets easier
to reason about. The classic pipeline fallback can reserve a fixed
maximum number of slots for each feature and collapse unused ones,
exactly as your current fallback collapses unavailable geometry.

This feels like the path from the present fixed chamfer to something
that looks deliberately modeled rather than merely rounded.

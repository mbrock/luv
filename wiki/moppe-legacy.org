#+title: The moppe legacy: lighting, post, and atmosphere worth transplanting
#+startup: overview

* What this page is
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: C4XGYX
:END:

Moppe (=~/src/moppe=) is the predecessor engine: a C++/Metal terrain world
with a complete cinematic rendering pipeline.  For luvcraft it plays three
roles at once.  It is *proof* that the ambitious end state runs well on our
actual machines; it is a *repository of tuned judgment calls* — hundreds of
smoothstep edges, blend weights, and bias constants that took real iteration
to find; and it is a *legacy to improve on*, because its answers are frozen
in MSL text and duplicated by convention, where luv holds the same material
as live, inspectable, redefinable Lisp objects.

The discipline for reading it follows #H7QK3M: distinguish mechanisms
observed in moppe's code, claims its comments make, and our reading of what
luvcraft should adopt or reject.  Moppe is a landmark like WebGPU is a
landmark — evidence, not authority.

* The post stack: what "finished-looking" turns out to be made of
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 8VA1EX
:END:

=moppe/shaders/metal/post.metal= is a complete answer to the question the
mockup raised: most of what reads as "high quality" is the treatment of the
frame after the scene is lit.  The observed pipeline, in order:

1. *Auto-exposure*: a probe pass averages a few wide taps of the HDR scene
   into a tiny target the CPU reads back; an adaptation loop tracks it.  The
   exposure scalar rides a uniform lane.  Notably the bloom bright pass sees
   the /exposed/ scene, so glow tracks adaptation in matching units.
2. *Bloom*: a soft-knee bright pass (=smoothstep (0.85, 1.35, luma)=) into a
   quarter-resolution chain; the 9-tap gaussian is done as five linear
   samples placed between texel pairs so hardware interpolation evaluates
   half the kernel.
3. *Lens character*: chromatic fringe only beyond the image circle's outer
   third; a warm veil, anamorphic streak, and three tinted ghosts on the
   sun, gated by a sun-visibility scalar that already folds in occlusion.
4. *ACES tonemap* (Narkowicz fit), then sRGB encode.
5. *Colorist grade*: warm print-film CDL, split-toned shadows/highlights,
   vibrance that saturates muted pixels faster than rich ones, luma-weighted
   animated grain, vignette.
6. *EDR path*: on capable displays it re-linearizes and lets only true HDR
   highlights rise above diffuse white, clamped to live display headroom.

Two readings for luvcraft.  First, the sequencing lesson: exposure and
tonemapping are the foundation everything else composes over, which is
exactly the order #SKHDR already commits to.  Second, the grade (step 5) is
where moppe's screenshots get their mood; it is cheap, entirely separable,
and every constant in it is an art parameter that in luv would be a live
uniform lane or a hot-replaceable material method rather than a recompile.

* Sun shafts: the highest-glamour transplant
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: BAP0QU
:END:

Moppe's =shafts_gather_fragment= marches each view ray at half resolution
through the *existing shadow map*, jittered by interleaved-gradient noise,
stopped by scene depth, and weights the gathered lit spans by a
Henyey-Greenstein forward lobe (g = 0.60) around the sun.  A separate pass
adds the filtered half-resolution result to the scene.

Luvcraft already owns both inputs: a stored shadow depth map (#2WX9PW) and
an offscreen scene with depth.  The technique needs no new world data, no
mesh changes, and no new shadow semantics — it is a second consumer of
products that exist.  God rays through block-tree canopies at a low sun are
plausibly the single largest visible-quality step per line of code on the
current path.  It does want the linear-HDR target first (#WLHDRB), so the
gathered light adds in sensible units.

* The judgment-call library in common.h
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 094ZB1
:END:

=moppe/shaders/metal/common.h= is the densest artifact: shared helpers whose
value is not the algorithm but the tuning.  Observed vocabulary worth
carrying over, roughly in order of immediate applicability:

- =moppe_hemisphere_light=: ambient split into warm earth bounce below and
  cool sky above (=mix= on normal.y).  One line; luvcraft's flat ambient
  term would gain depth immediately.
- =moppe_warmed_fog=: fog warmed toward the sun by two Mie-ish lobes and
  cooled faintly opposite it (Rayleigh backscatter), so panning across the
  horizon reads as one atmosphere.  Direct upgrade to the block material's
  single fog colour.
- =moppe_sky_radiance=: a deliberately cheap analytic environment radiance
  varying with reflected direction — zenith/horizon palette plus a solar
  highlight — used by water.  This is the concrete form of the "analytic
  sky as environment map" idea for crystal and snow specular: no cubemaps,
  just the sky function evaluated along the reflected ray.
- =moppe_sun_visibility=: the tuned PCF recipe — centre-weighted five taps,
  =pow 1.3= shaping, shadow influence fading with fog distance.  Luvcraft's
  17-tap disk (#UDVPDW) is already beyond it spatially; the fog fade and
  response shaping remain worth stealing.
- =moppe_cloud_transmission=: cloud /shadows/ by projecting each world point
  along the sun onto one world-stable layer and sampling a cheap wave
  field.  Ground, trees, and water all darken under the same drifting
  shape.  Relevant the day luvcraft's sky grows clouds.
- Wind on three coupled clocks (gust drives bough drives flutter), foliage
  back-transmission, and world-stable hashed alpha coverage: not yet
  relevant to rigid blocks, but the answers are written down for when
  foliage stops being rigid.

The critique that motivates doing this in luv rather than by porting text:
the =daylight= / =golden= smoothstep pair is independently recomputed in at
least four places (sky atmosphere, cloud lighting, warmed fog, sky
radiance).  It stays consistent by convention only.  In luv that pair is one
shader-abstraction definition (#DXYHT4 machinery) spliced wherever needed,
and retuning it is one live redefinition.

* Sky, temporal, and mesh shaders: the wider proof
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: KK6M0T
:END:

Three further observations bound what our hardware and stack can do.

- =sky.metal= renders a three-stop atmosphere (zenith, mid band, horizon),
  a golden-hour band, two Mie lobes, an fBm cumulus deck plus sheared
  cirrus, silver linings on thin sun-facing edges, stars, dither, and a
  horizon blended into the exact fog colour.  Luvcraft's sky (#SKDYCY) is
  the same shape at smaller scale; the cloud material is the natural next
  chapter and stays pure sky-shader mathematics.
- Every scene shader emits motion vectors and a reactive mask
  (=MoppeTemporalOutput=) feeding MetalFX temporal upscaling.  Relevant to
  #GKEHPF: moppe's answer to temporal shimmer was full temporal
  reconstruction; luvcraft is right to first try comparison-sampler
  filtering, but the motion-vector plumbing pattern is documented here if
  evidence ever demands the heavier answer.
- The forest shadow pass runs as a Metal *mesh shader* pipeline
  (=MTLMeshRenderPipelineDescriptor=, object + mesh functions).  This is
  existence proof that mesh shaders work today on our machines — through
  Metal.  MoltenVK does not expose =VK_EXT_mesh_shader=, so the current
  Vulkan HAL cannot reach them; see #NABRMK.

* A Metal HAL beside the Vulkan HAL
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: NABRMK
:END:

The mesh-shader gap makes a second backend more than an aesthetic
preference: the Vulkan-over-MoltenVK path structurally cannot reach a
hardware capability every development machine has.  Reading moppe alongside
luv's stack suggests the port is smaller than it first appears.

- The shader story is the easy half, unusually.  Luv's
  =shader-specification= objects are already the typed semantic graph above
  SPIR-V.  An MSL backend can lower that graph directly to target-specific
  C-like text rather than translating the already SPIR-V-shaped module.
  Material specifications stay shared; target lowering and its provenance
  product differ.  #LVKEYO makes that boundary precise.
- The API choreography — pipeline descriptors, residency, drawable
  acquisition, depth formats, MetalFX — is worked out in
  =moppe/render/metal/metal_renderer.mm= (~7000 lines of Objective-C++,
  itself an argument for driving the Objective-C runtime from Lisp
  instead).  #JSKLEI develops the bridge as a foreign object system rather
  than a list of Metal wrappers.  Moppe also has a WebGPU renderer, giving two
  HAL shapes to compare against #L7C4GM-adjacent boundary questions.
- The target is Metal 4 directly, not Metal 3 with Metal 4 later.  An
  earlier draft of this page advised the reverse; #HXUGEA records why that
  caution was backwards for this codebase.  All development machines run
  macOS 26+, and no legacy support is wanted.
- The post stack (#8VA1EX) is deliberately HAL-agnostic: fullscreen passes
  over offscreen textures work identically on the Vulkan path today and
  survive a Metal port untouched.  Nothing cinematic needs to wait.

* Metal 4's explicit model matches the Vulkan layer
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: HXUGEA
:END:

The decisive question for a second HAL is not the API's age but whose
contract matches the machinery luv already owns.  Metal 4's paradigm shift
— explicit memory, explicit synchronization, no automatic hazard tracking
— asks the application to provide exactly what luv's Vulkan layer already
provides, where Metal 3's implicit model would duplicate or fight it.

Observed correspondences:

- Command allocators may be =reset= only after their commands retire on
  the GPU, with the application proving retirement.  Luv's per-queue
  completion frontier is precisely that proof, already trusted to gate
  destruction and reuse on the Vulkan path.
- =MTLSharedEvent= with monotonic signaled values is nearly one-to-one
  with the timeline semaphores the frontier is built on.
- Metal 4 is "concurrent by default" with an explicit stage-to-stage
  barrier API; luv already encodes explicit image transitions rather than
  relying on driver tracking.
- =MTL4ArgumentTable= binds resources by 64-bit GPU resource IDs and GPU
  addresses with offset arithmetic — closer to the descriptor-set/bind
  group model luv already generalized than Metal 3's per-draw setter
  state.
- Metal 4 command buffers do not retain resources; the application keeps
  them alive until execution completes.  That is luv's explicit-destroy
  contract with frontier-deferred reclamation, stated as an API rule.
- =MTLResidencySet= committed once and attached at queue scope matches
  residency as a session-level concern rather than per-encoder chatter.

The compilation story is the strongest specific attraction for a live
system.  =MTL4Compiler= makes compilation an explicit, allocatable object
separate from the device: runtime library creation from source, async
pipeline builds with QoS, function-constant specialization as data, and
pipeline serialization for warm starts.  The live-pipeline artifact can
own a compiler instance and keep its current transactional
last-known-good behavior, with Lisp-emitted MSL text entering compilation
the same way Lisp-assembled SPIR-V does today.  Specialized function
descriptors offer a natural home for material parameters worth freezing
into pipeline variants instead of spending uniform lanes.

What Metal 4 does not give back is a safety net: nothing tracks hazards
for a HAL that forgets a barrier.  For luv this is the correct trade —
the Vulkan layer already assumes that responsibility, and a backend that
silently re-tracked hazards underneath the frontier would obscure the
very guarantees the frontier exists to make inspectable.

#+title: HLSL, shader programs, and luv-shaderc
#+startup: overview

* One shader source for Metal and Direct3D renderers outside Luv
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: LSVW2S
:END:

The mathematical shader language (#S4H8DR) now has a third textual lowering
and a consumer outside the live image.  =luv/hlsl= lowers the same typed graph
to HLSL for DXC, and =luv-shaderc= compiles named programs ahead of time into
MSL, HLSL, and the reflection a native C++ renderer builds its pipelines from.
The first such renderer is moppe's NHAL: Metal 4 on Apple platforms and
Direct3D 12 on an Xbox Series in Developer Mode, shader model 6.4 at most.

#+begin_example
shader source file (DEFINE-SHADER, DEFINE-SHADER-PROGRAM)
        |
        | load in LUV.SHADER-USER, LINK-SHADER-PROGRAM
        v
shader-program-linkage -- resources in binding families, stage interface
        |
        +-- compile-msl  --> NAME.STAGE.metal
        +-- compile-hlsl --> NAME.STAGE.hlsl   (DXC: vs_6_0 / ps_6_0 / cs_6_0)
        +-- program-json --> NAME.json
        `-- program-header -> NAME.hh          (moppe/nhal/reflection.hh)
#+end_example

#VH2TIP describes the lowering, #7A2ONF the program and its binding families,
#1I6G0R the compiler and its package, #CPIOH5, #54MBHW, and #UABDNH the
fragment, texture, and compute vocabulary moppe's renderer asked for, and
#4TZZRS what a real-time renderer still cannot say in the language.

* The HLSL lowering is a sibling of the MSL one
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: VH2TIP
:END:

[[file:../hal/d3d12/hlsl/lowering.lisp][=hal/d3d12/hlsl/lowering.lisp=]] mirrors the MSL lowering: an
=hlsl-document= of structures, resource declarations, and one entry point,
with every rendered expression occurrence retained against its shader
expression and the same plain-language quantity comments, which now live in
[[file:../hal/shader/semantics.lisp][=hal/shader/semantics.lisp=]] for both.  The deliberate dialect choices:

- Resources follow the families of #7A2ONF.  A uniform block is a structure
  inside =cbuffer NameBlock : register(bN)=, so members read =block.member=
  as in MSL; a read-only storage buffer is a =StructuredBuffer= at =tN,
  space0=, a read-write one an =RWStructuredBuffer= at =uN=; textures are
  =Texture2D= at =tN, space1=, so buffer and texture numbers never collide;
  a sampler is =SamplerState=, or =SamplerComparisonState= when the program
  compares depth through it.
- Clip Y is negated at the position output exactly as MSL and WGSL do: the
  shared camera graph keeps Vulkan's framebuffer orientation, and Direct3D,
  like Metal, points clip Y up with depth 0..1.  A shader author therefore
  writes Vulkan-style clip positions for every target.
- =sample= is =Sample= in pixel shaders and =SampleLevel(..., 0)= elsewhere;
  =sample-compare= is always =SampleCmpLevelZero=, legal in every stage and in
  divergent code, and equal to Metal's comparison for single-level shadow
  maps.  A depth texture is =Texture2D<float>=, widened to the language's vec4
  by a scalar cast, as MSL's =float4(depth)= does.
- =mix= is =lerp=, =fract= =frac=, the derivatives =ddx= / =ddy=, and =signum=
  casts HLSL's integer =sign= back to its operand type.  Conditionals always
  have a scalar condition, so =?:= means the same under HLSL 2018 and 2021.
- Integer varyings are =nointerpolation=.  The pixel input structure starts
  with =SV_Position= and mirrors the vertex outputs location by location,
  because Direct3D links stages by signature layout, not only by semantic.

=make hlsl-validate= discovers every application vertex and fragment shader
(98 of them), lowers each, and compiles it with DXC with warnings as errors;
DXC is in the development shells for this.  The unit claims in
[[file:../hal/d3d12/hlsl/tests.lisp][=hal/d3d12/hlsl/tests.lisp=]] compile their probes with DXC too.

* Programs link stages in Metal's and Direct3D's binding families
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 7A2ONF
:END:

=define-shader-program= in [[file:../hal/shader/program.lisp][=hal/shader/program.lisp=]] names the stages one pipeline
runs: vertex and fragment, vertex alone for a depth pass, or compute.  Its
values name =define-shader= functions, called when the program is linked, so
redefining a stage reaches the program.

=link-shader-resources= keys resources by Vulkan's (set, binding).  Metal and
Direct3D number buffers, textures, and samplers independently, so
=link-shader-program= keys them by (family, binding) instead: a uniform block
and a texture may both be binding 0, while a uniform block and a storage
buffer share Metal's one buffer index space and collide.  It also checks the
inter-stage interface (type and interpolation at each fragment location),
dense colour outputs, and which samplers compare depth, rejecting a sampler
used both ways because HLSL gives the two uses different types.  The MSL and
HLSL lowerings reject a shared index within one stage as well.

* Fragment stages see their pixel and may discard it
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: CPIOH5
:END:

A fragment stage may declare four more built-ins and one statement:

#+begin_src lisp
:inputs ((pixel :vec4 :built-in :frag-coord)
         (front :bool :built-in :front-facing)
         (sample :uint :built-in :sample-index))
:outputs ((color :vec4 :location 0)
          (depth :float :built-in :frag-depth))
...
(when (< (swizzle base :a) 0.5) (discard))
#+end_src

=:frag-coord= is Vulkan's =FragCoord= and Metal's =[[position]]=: x and y in
framebuffer pixels from the top-left corner at pixel centres (0.5, 0.5 is the
first pixel), z the fragment's depth, and w the reciprocal of its clip w.
Direct3D's =SV_Position= agrees except that its w is the clip w itself, so the
HLSL lowering reads =float4(p.xyz, 1.0f / p.w)= from the one =SV_Position=
every pixel input structure already begins with.  The y convention needs no
correction anywhere: the clip-Y negation of #VH2TIP makes all three put the
same image in the same framebuffer rows.

=:front-facing= is =SV_IsFrontFace=, =[[front_facing]]=, =FrontFacing=; which
winding is front is pipeline state.  Vulkan measures winding in its y-down
framebuffer and Metal and Direct3D as seen with y up, so a host that wants
one triangle to be front everywhere declares opposite windings on Vulkan.
=:sample-index= (=SV_SampleIndex=, =[[sample_id]]=, a flat =SampleId= with
=SampleRateShading=) runs the stage per sample.  =:frag-depth= is =SV_Depth=,
=[[depth(any)]]=, or =FragDepth= with =DepthReplacing=, in the same 0..1
(reversed or not) as the depth buffer.  =discard= is =discard;=,
=discard_fragment()=, and =OpKill=; WGSL gets these too.

* Textures have dimensions; storage textures have formats
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 54MBHW
:END:

Beside =:texture-2d=, =:depth-texture-2d=, and =:uint-texture-2d= there are
=:texture-2d-array=, =:depth-texture-2d-array=, =:texture-cube=, and
=:texture-3d=: =Texture2DArray<…>=, =TextureCube=, =Texture3D= at =tN,
space1=, and =texture2d_array=, =depth2d_array=, =texturecube=, =texture3d= at
=[[texture(N)]]=.  Every texture operator takes the texture, then a sampler
when it samples, then the coordinate (vec2, or vec3 for a cube's direction or
a volume's UVW); an array's layer, a =:uint=, always follows the coordinate;
then come the operator's own operands:

| operator | after the coordinate | result |
|----------+----------------------+--------|
| =sample= | | the sample |
| =sample-level= | lod | explicit level |
| =sample-bias= | bias | fragments only |
| =sample-grad= | ddx ddy (coordinate type) | explicit gradients |
| =sample-compare= | depth reference | float, 2D and 2D-array depth |
| =gather= | | vec4 of the four red texels (uvec4 for uint) |
| =gather-compare= | depth reference | vec4 of four comparisons |
| =texel-load= | [level] (=:uint=) | exact texel; no cubes |
| =texture-size= | (texture [level] only) | uvec2, uvec3 with layers or depth |

In HLSL a layer becomes the last float of the coordinate and =texture-size=
is =GetDimensions= into temporaries; MSL passes the layer as its own argument
and wraps level, bias, and gradients in =level()=, =bias()=, and
=gradient2d/cube/3d()=; SPIR-V appends the layer as a float (or an unsigned
texel coordinate) and uses =Lod=, =Bias=, and =Grad= image operands,
=OpImageGather=, =OpImageDrefGather=, and =OpImageQuerySizeLod= (with
=ImageQuery=).  =gather-compare= needs a comparison sampler, as
=sample-compare= does.

A storage texture is declared with a format:

#+begin_src lisp
(heat :read-write-texture-2d :binding 0 :format :r32f)
...
(set-texel heat xy (vec4 (+ (swizzle (texel-load heat xy) :x) 1.0) 0.0 0.0 0.0))
#+end_src

The formats are =:rgba32f=, =:rgba16f=, =:rgba8= (unsigned normalized),
=:r32f=, and =:r32ui=; texels are vec4 (uvec4 for =:r32ui=), and a
one-channel format reads (r, 0, 0, 1) and stores the first component.  Storage
textures are a binding family of their own (#7A2ONF): Metal
=texture2d<…, access::read_write>= at =[[texture(16 + N)]]=, past the sixteen
sampled textures; Direct3D =RWTexture2D<…>= at =uN, space1=, apart from the
read-write buffers' =uN, space0=; SPIR-V a storage image with the declared
format, so no =StorageImageReadWithoutFormat=.  =texel-load= and
=set-texel= work in compute and fragment stages.  Reading the four-channel
formats needs Direct3D's typed UAV loads of additional formats and Metal's
tier-2 read-write textures, which the Xbox Series and Apple silicon have.

* Compute workgroups share memory, meet at barriers, and count atomically
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: UABDNH
:END:

A compute stage may declare workgroup arrays, 16 KiB in all (Vulkan's
guaranteed minimum; Direct3D and Metal allow 32):

#+begin_src lisp
(define-shader reduce-compute
    (:stage :compute :workgroup-size (64 1 1)
     :inputs ((cell :uvec3 :built-in :global-invocation-id)
              (local :uint :built-in :local-invocation-index)
              (lane :uint :built-in :wave-lane-index))
     :shared ((partial :float 64))
     :resources (...))
  ...)
#+end_src

=(shared-element partial i)= reads one and =(set-shared-element partial i v)=
stores one; they are =groupshared=, kernel-scope =threadgroup= variables, and
=Workgroup= storage.  =(workgroup-barrier)= is
=GroupMemoryBarrierWithGroupSync=, =threadgroup_barrier(mem_threadgroup)=, and
=OpControlBarrier= at workgroup scope with workgroup memory semantics;
=(storage-barrier)= also orders storage buffers and textures
(=AllMemoryBarrierWithGroupSync=, device and texture memory flags, device
scope).  Both must stand in workgroup-uniform control flow (#X4ZZ92).

=atomic-add=, =atomic-min=, =atomic-max=, =atomic-and=, =atomic-or=,
=atomic-xor=, =atomic-exchange= (target index value) and
=atomic-compare-exchange= (target index comparand value) work on =:uint=
elements of read-write storage buffers and workgroup arrays, are relaxed, and
return the element's previous value.  HLSL uses the =Interlocked= functions on
=RWStructuredBuffer<uint>= elements; SPIR-V =OpAtomic*= at device or
workgroup scope.  Metal needs an atomic type, so a buffer or workgroup array
that any atomic touches is =atomic_uint= in that stage and every plain read
and store of it becomes a relaxed =atomic_load_explicit= or
=atomic_store_explicit=.  Metal's only compare-exchange is weak; the lowering
retries exactly its spurious failures so the result means what it means
elsewhere.

Waves: the built-ins =:wave-lane-index= and =:wave-lane-count=, and
=wave-active-sum=, =wave-prefix-sum= (exclusive), and =wave-ballot= (a uvec4
mask) are shader model 6.0's =Wave= intrinsics, MSL's simdgroup attributes,
=simd_sum=, =simd_prefix_exclusive_sum=, and =simd_ballot=, and SPIR-V 1.3's
=GroupNonUniform= operations, which raise only the modules that use them to
Vulkan 1.1.  Their results depend on which lanes are active, so they belong in
uniform control flow as well, though that is not checked.

[[file:../hal/shaderc/examples/instance-culling.lisp][=examples/instance-culling.lisp=]] is the first program that needs this: one
invocation per instance tests a bounding sphere against six frustum planes and
appends visible instance indices through an atomic count kept in the indexed
indirect-draw argument record, whose five words Direct3D's
=D3D12_DRAW_INDEXED_ARGUMENTS= and Metal's
=MTLDrawIndexedPrimitivesIndirectArguments= order the same way.

* luv-shaderc writes stages, a manifest, and a C++ header
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 1I6G0R
:END:

#+begin_example
nix run github:mbrock/luv#luv-shaderc -- --out DIR [--target msl] [--target hlsl] FILE.lisp...
#+end_example

[[file:../hal/shaderc/shaderc.lisp][=hal/shaderc/=]] reads each file in =LUV.SHADER-USER= (Common Lisp plus the
shader language, unless the file says =in-package=), links every program, and
checks moppe's binding contract (=docs/nhal.md= there): buffer and texture
bindings below 16, descriptor set 0, the standard sampler set by number (3 is
the comparison sampler), and no vertex attributes, since vertex stages pull
from storage buffers.  For a program =NAME= it writes =NAME.STAGE.metal=,
=NAME.STAGE.hlsl=, =NAME.json=, and =NAME.hh=; every entry point is
=name_stage= in both languages.  Errors name the file, the failing form's
line, the program and stage, the language's reason, and the source form, and
exit nonzero.  [[file:../hal/shaderc/examples/textured-instances.lisp][=examples/textured-instances.lisp=]] pulls vertices and
instances, samples a texture, and compares a shadow map;
[[file:../hal/shaderc/examples/particle-advance.lisp][=examples/particle-advance.lisp=]] is a compute program, and
[[file:../hal/shaderc/examples/particle-swarm.lisp][=examples/particle-swarm.lisp=]] one over structured particles with a mat4
uniform, and [[file:../hal/shaderc/examples/instance-culling.lisp][=examples/instance-culling.lisp=]] culls instances on the GPU
(#UABDNH).  The tests compile them with Metal, DXC, spirv-val, and the C++
compiler.

The resource kinds in the manifest and header are the enumerators of moppe's
=ResourceKind=, among them =texture_2d_array=, =depth_texture_2d_array=,
=texture_cube=, =texture_3d=, and =read_write_texture_2d=; a storage texture's
manifest entry also names its =format=, its family =storage_texture=, and its
Metal index =[[texture(16 + N)]]=.  A compute program's header carries its
workgroup size in the =Program= value itself, =.workgroup_size = {64, 1, 1}=.
[[file:../hal/shaderc/fixtures/moppe/nhal/reflection.hh][=fixtures/moppe/nhal/reflection.hh=]] is a copy of moppe's header for those
tests; refresh it when moppe's changes.

The flake's =packages.<system>.luv-shaderc= builds from the shader sources
alone with an SBCL holding only Closer-MOP, through
[[file:../scripts/build-luv-shaderc.lisp][=scripts/build-luv-shaderc.lisp=]] (also =make luv-shaderc=).  The image is a
compressed core whose closure is itself and zstd: about 14 MiB, with no SDL,
Vulkan, Metal, or McCLIM.

* TODO What a real-time renderer still cannot say
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 4TZZRS
:END:

Compute stages and read-write storage buffers exist (=:access :read-write=,
=set-buffer-element=, =:workgroup-size=, =:global-invocation-id=), and so do
signed integers, booleans, and bit operations (#CAI3RP), column-major
matrices, which HLSL represents transposed (#QEHEEE), structures with one
host layout, written into the header as asserted C++ structures (#V16OXI),
fragment built-ins (#CPIOH5), the texture kinds and storage textures of
#54MBHW, and workgroup memory, barriers, atomics, and waves (#UABDNH); but a
renderer of moppe's kind still lacks, roughly in order of need:

- vec3 storage elements and structure fields (deliberately: pad them), and
  fixed-size arrays as values or fields;
- uniform members other than vec4 lanes and mat4s (deliberately, for the
  layout contract), arrays in uniform blocks, and push or root constants;
- blending-relevant outputs beyond colour, and per-sample interpolation of
  ordinary inputs;
- cube arrays and depth cubes, multisampled textures, texel offsets on
  gathers and samples, gathers of other channels, and bindless tables;
- storage textures other than 2D, atomics on storage textures or on signed
  integers, and loops a barrier can stand in (folds are expressions, so a
  barrier cannot repeat);
- the group count in HLSL, which has no system value for it;
- the vertex =:location= inputs exist but are outside NHAL's contract.

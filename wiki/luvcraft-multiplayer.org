#+title: Two players in one Luvcraft world
#+startup: overview

Luvcraft on Luft (#J3WZZS) is played mostly by two people on one local
network: one machine hosts the world and its save, another joins it.  Both
play at full frame rate, both see every block, and both meet the same
turtles, balls, sky, and companions.  This page is the design and the work
plan.  Moppe already plays two players over the LAN (its commit =637d1754=,
=companion_link=); its lessons carry over for bodies, but a block world must
also agree on an unbounded, ordered set of edits, which moppe never had to.

* A host sequences the world; each player owns a body
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 8EZK9F
:END:

Block edits do not merge: two people filling one cell need exactly one
winner, and the save has one home.  So the host runs a /sequencer/ that
gives every authored event its place in one journal (#3F0SCZ), while each
player remains authoritative for their own body, look, and gait, which
travel as 30 Hz pose snapshots, as in moppe.

| thing                          | authority                     | travels as                        |
|--------------------------------+-------------------------------+-----------------------------------|
| terrain baseline               | the recipe                    | a world tag checked on join       |
| block edits                    | the host's sequencer          | reliable ordered events           |
| a player's body, look, gait    | that player                   | unreliable pose snapshots         |
| inventory and hotbar           | the player, from confirmed events | the host's players table      |
| turtles, balls, springs, sky   | the host                      | entity snapshots                  |
| companions                     | the host (provider and tools) | speech and journey events         |
| particles and sounds           | derived from confirmed events | nothing                           |

On a LAN the round trip is a few milliseconds, so only one's own body is
predicted.  An edit appears when the host confirms it.  The host's own edits
take the same path through its local sequencer, so playing alone exercises
the multiplayer path.  Edit prediction becomes worth having only for
tailnet play, and the checked before/after values of #3F0SCZ make it a
small addition then.

* The game is a world, its players, and their views
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 32DUBY
:END:

Today the =luvcraft-viewer= application frame is the game: shared
populations, one player's body and inventory, and that player's camera live
in one CLIM object.  Multiplayer needs three owners.

- A /world/ owns the source, the journal, the streaming scene, the
  =world-simulation=, the populations, the sky clock, and a players table.
  It runs without a window.
- A /player/ owns an identity, a character and body, an inventory and
  hotbar, the held block, creative mode, and rider intent; a remote player
  also owns its snapshot track (#VM5TN6).
- A /view/ owns a camera, controls, overlays, particles, and a reference to
  its world and local player.

The game-host protocols become forwards to the world, and two worlds can
run in one image for tests.

* One journal of events is the authority for authored change
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 3F0SCZ
:END:

Every authored change is an event: sequence, tick, actor, transaction,
site, before, after, cause, as #OVEP9A proposes.  Before and after are
block identities (#FBU3TF), not visual placements.  The sequencer applies
an event only if its before still holds, and otherwise rejects it with a
reason; a transaction applies whole.  An actor is a principal: a player, an
inhabitant, or an import.

Luft's =world-source-edits= and Luvcraft's =block-edit-overlay= become
derived views of this journal rather than two ledgers kept in step.  Undo
belongs to an actor: it appends the inverse of that actor's own last event
when its after still holds, so building together never undoes someone
else's work.

* A sequenced event is never refused
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: QQT86P
:END:

=edit-streaming-scene-cell= answers =:busy= while a mesh cohort is in
flight.  A click may see that, but a sequenced event has already happened.
The scene owner keeps a queue of sequenced events and applies them at a
frame boundary; an in-flight cohort is rescheduled as the next edit's
cohort would be.

* Blocks need names that fit on the wire
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: FBU3TF
:END:

An event names its before and after.  Films are block-kind instances
outside =*block-kinds*=, so the material vocabulary does not close and a
block has no integer name.  #R7MAN5 closes it: one static film material,
with per-film state in sparse site-keyed entities.  A block identity is then
a vocabulary offset and an optional entity.  The join handshake carries the
vocabulary table, and the guest resolves it into a remap (#FTDOCV), the same
seam a save migration uses.

* Input is a value per tick, on a phase-locked clock
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 3QHL50
:END:

The viewer makes one plain input frame per tick -- movement axes, look
delta, jump and flight edges -- and that frame steers the local player.  The
clock adopts moppe's =simulation_clock=: 120 Hz steps rounded to the
nearest step and at most six steps of catch-up.  Tick numbers are the
shared time base that snapshots carry, and recorded input frames are the
tapes that soak bots replay.

* Interest, not focus
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: I6O4J6
:END:

A streaming scene follows one focus.  The host must also simulate a turtle
beside a distant guest, so the scene keeps a set of interest foci, one per
player the host simulates for, and keeps collision residency for their
union.  Meshes, light, and GPU publication still follow only the local
camera.  A guest's interest is its own focus; remote players outside it are
drawn from snapshots and need no collision.  This answers the
"distant-actor residency" left open by the simulation boundary of the Luft page.

* Peers agree on the world by hash, chunk by chunk
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: RELI34
:END:

A world tag hashes the generator version, the recipe, and the vocabulary
digest; every message carries it, and a peer in another world is told so.
Each resident chunk also has a content hash over its fibers and materials
after edits, which peers compare lazily.  Luvcraft's generator calls
transcendental functions, so macOS and Linux may disagree; a mismatch makes
the host ship that chunk's content and is logged as a determinism bug, as
moppe's world fingerprint does for its own stages.

** Seed 121's fingerprint on Linux
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: QTXNY0
:END:

=terrain:world-source-tag= hashes the recipe that
=terrain:world-source-recipe= returns -- for Luvcraft,
=:luvcraft-little-world=, =+luvcraft-generator-version+=, seed, relief,
height, little class, offset, and domain -- with
=terrain:material-vocabulary-digest=, the placement names, kind names, and
attachment forms in offset order.  =terrain:resident-cell-chunk-hash=
hashes a resident chunk's key, fiber words, material cells by ascending
site, and attachments by ascending clearance cell; a live-edited chunk and
one raised again from the recipe and its edits hash alike.  Both are MD5
over canonical octets: a checksum between honest peers, not a defence.

without a window.  On Linux x86-64 (chapel, SBCL 2.6.7), twice in fresh
processes:

The chunk generator itself calls no libm function: Luvcraft's terrain is
integer coordinate hashes and IEEE double =+=, =-=, =*=, and =/=, and the
birthday meadow adds only =sqrt=, which IEEE rounds exactly.  The one libm
call on the way is =atan= in =little-world-summit-yaw=, which only turns
the spawn's yaw, printed above as a single float.  The birthday gazebo's
=cos= and =sin= (=luvcraft/birthday/gazebo.lisp=) choose cells by rounding,
but the gazebo arrives as edits, so it travels with the journal rather than
being regenerated.  The test =seed-121-hashes-as-it-did-on-linux= pins the
tag and chunk (16, 16), so =make test= on a Mac is the comparison.

* The wire: a sans-IO codec over two channels
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: A8LXLK
:END:

Messages are pure encode/decode functions over octet vectors, tested without
sockets, as =mqtt/= is.  Every message carries a magic, a protocol version,
the world tag, and a session.  Decoding is strict: finite numbers, unit
directions, sites inside the domain, offsets inside the vocabulary.

One TCP connection carries the reliable stream -- handshake, catch-up,
events, requests, chat -- and one UDP socket carries pose and entity
snapshots, falling back to TCP when no datagram arrives.  One network
worker owns both sockets and posts decoded messages to a mailbox that the
world drains at a frame boundary.  Discovery is a LAN broadcast, an explicit
=--join host=, and optionally a retained session entry in the tailnet lobby.

* Joining is catch-up, then subscription
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 2SGDU2
:END:

The guest says hello with its protocol, stable player identity, and name.
The host welcomes it with the session, recipe, vocabulary table, journal
watermark N, the edit set compacted at N, an entity snapshot, and the
guest's player record and resume pose.  The guest raises the world from the
recipe, installs the edit set, and receives events strictly after N: the
checked handoff of #8DQ18B, which neither drops nor repeats an event.

* A remote player is drawn a little behind time
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: VM5TN6
:END:

Moppe's =CompanionTrack= is the model: thirty-two snapshots, an adaptive
playback delay of 0.05 to 0.3 seconds steered toward the sender's tick, a
spline between snapshots, 0.2 seconds of extrapolation, eased corrections,
and a leap detector for teleports.  The body uses the skinned creature
pipeline with a held block and a nameplate.  Remote bodies also enter local
physics read-only, so balls bounce off them and nobody places a block
inside them.

* A save is recipe, vocabulary, journal, and players
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: Y3S82T
:END:

The host's save becomes format 2: the recipe, the vocabulary table, the
edit set compacted at a watermark with the recent journal tail, a players
table of last pose, inventory, and hotbar by stable identity, and the
populations.  Format 1 loads through the remap and is written as format 2.
Legacy Luvcraft no longer reads what this game writes; that was decided
deliberately.  A guest's own saves are untouched by joining.

** DONE Keep one journal of events, and queue sequenced edits
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 48I94O
:END:

/Intent./ Realize #3F0SCZ and #QQT86P in the terrain engine, with
actor-owned undo.

/Done when./ Edits are journal events with checked before values, the two
ledgers are derived views, a queued event survives a busy cohort, and two
actors' interleaved edits undo independently.

/Evidence./ A [[lisp:luft.terrain:world-journal]] holds
[[lisp:luft.terrain:world-event]]s, and [[lisp:luft.terrain:sequence-world-events]]
records a transaction only when every change's before is the site's value at
the journal head, else names the reason (=:stale-before=, =:nonresident=,
=:attachment=, ...).  A source scene shares its source's journal, and
=world-source-edits= is the journal's latest value per site.  Recorded events
wait in the scene's queue until [[lisp:luft.terrain:apply-queued-world-events]]
finds no cohort in flight; a click whose before a queued event has changed is
stale.  [[lisp:luft.terrain:undo-world-events]] inverts the actor's own latest
transaction and forgets one that someone has since changed; the atelier's
undo goes through it as actor =:local=.  Values are opaque to the journal and
painted through [[lisp:luft.terrain:world-value-placement]], so a block
identity can replace a placement.  In Luvcraft on Luft the values are #JH74JS's block
identities, so a film's entity rides in its event, and
=block-edit-overlay= is a view brought up to the journal's head from the
events past its watermark.  A save's edit set is compacted at the head, so
its =:watermark= is the head and its =:journal= tail is empty; loading
installs that set at the watermark, and sequence numbers continue.

** DONE Speak the wire protocol
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: H6A8P4
:END:

/Intent./ Realize #A8LXLK as its own system: the codec, both channels, the
network worker and mailbox, discovery, and an in-memory loopback transport.

/Evidence./ =luvcraft-wire= is a codec of typed records and fourteen

snapshots by datagram, or by the stream when datagrams are off.  Open:

are placeholders for #JH74JS, #7V3536, and #JHWV75 to settle; there is no
reconnection or lobby entry yet.

/Done when./ Every message round-trips and malformed input is refused; two
endpoints in one image find each other by broadcast and by name, and trade
reliable and unreliable messages over real sockets and over loopback.

** DONE Steer players by input frames on a phase-locked clock
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: X56QS3
:END:

/Intent./ Realize #3QHL50 after #7V3536 lands.

/Done when./ The local player is steered only by input frames, the clock
rounds to the nearest step, and a recorded tape replays the same walk.

/Evidence./ A [[lisp:luft.terrain:input-frame]] holds forward and right axes
along the character's own look, yaw and pitch deltas, jump and flight edges,
and a vertical urge.  [[lisp:luft.terrain:apply-input-frame]] is the only
steering path: the look turns first, and the walking direction follows it.
The viewer's =take-viewer-input-frame= reads held keys and the camera, and it
spends each press in exactly one frame.  The
=advance-world-simulation= =:before-tick= hook calls it once per tick.  A
ride, which moves once a frame, takes one frame each frame.  Luvcraft's
[[lisp:luvcraft.luft.game:steer-luvcraft-player]] feeds the same axes to the
player's rider intent, so reins are input frames too.  The clock is moppe's:
=consume-simulation-time= rounds to the nearest tick, runs at most six, and
discards the rest.  [[lisp:luft.terrain:simulation-tick]] is the integer time
base; =simulation-clock= is derived from it, and =simulation-phase= stays
within half a tick.  Interpolation sits half a tick behind elapsed time.  Tests
replay moppe's cadence, jitter, and stall cases, and a tape recorded through a
live viewer over irregular frames, with a jump, a strafe, and a turn, replays
to an identical position, velocity, and look.  Live play ran at 120.3 ticks/s.
In it, walking followed the look, a jump peaked at 1.65 cells (9²/48 = 1.69),
flight rose and hovered, a turtle turned and walked under D and W, and mouse
look turned the character's look with the camera.

/Open./ The camera still leads: the frame's look delta is the camera minus the
character's look, so a remote or replayed player's look is the character's,
not a camera's.  Tapes are in-memory vectors with no file format yet.

/Intent./ Phase 1: join #8EZK9F, #2SGDU2, and #VM5TN6 into a host and a

/Done when./ Two worlds in one image pass the join, catch-up, conflict,

/Evidence./ [[lisp:luvcraft.luft.game:host-luvcraft-world]] opens a session

simulation tick. Each remote player gets a
[[lisp:luvcraft.luft.game:remote-track]], ported from moppe's
CompanionTrack, and is drawn as the wizard figure. Chunk hashes are compared
at equal watermarks on a background thread, and any mismatch is logged.

=./sly play --host= and =./sly --lisp guest play --join localhost=. The game
port is 47475 on TCP and UDP. Discovery beacons use 47474. Open both on a
firewall.

The loopback tests cover:
- join;
- catch-up while the host builds;
- two players filling one cell, where one wins and the journals stay equal;
- per-player undo;
- leaving and rejoining where the player left;
- pose ticks;
- the remote track;
- a logged hash mismatch.

Two images on one Mac built a planks-and-bricks house with a wool roof. Both
journal heads reached 72, and every common chunk hash matched (764 and 607
comparisons, none different).

Live play exposed two terrain bugs, and both are fixed with regression
tests:
- A peer's event could arrive while residency moved. The queue now waits
  for a coherent scene.
- A guest could edit a chunk outside the host's collision radius. Occupancy
  there is now read from the chunk's fibers.

Still to do: a skinned body with a held block, remote bodies in local
physics, and a shared clock of day (each window keeps its own).

*** DONE Keep collision around every player
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 7CY5VW
:END:

/Intent./ Realize #I6O4J6, and settle the seam-falling turtles, the
outside-collision edit of #OLWSWF, and the teleport that fell through the
ground.

/Evidence./ A streaming scene keeps interest foci by player id
([[lisp:luft.terrain:set-streaming-scene-interest]],
[[lisp:luft.terrain:remove-streaming-scene-interest]]).  Each retarget
demands the gameplay radius around the local focus and every interest
focus, and installs collision for each of those chunks as soon as it is
resident ([[lisp:luft.terrain:install-streaming-collision]]); the mesh
window, light, and publication still follow only the local focus.  The
host's session sets a guest's focus from each of its poses and removes it
when the guest leaves; a guest declares none.

/Root cause./ More chunks are resident than collide: the store holds the
whole visible window (radius 3 in Luvcraft) and its guard, while collision
covers only the gameplay radius 1.  Turtles, companions, and the player
were frozen only off /resident/ chunks, so one standing on a chunk that was
resident but had no collision -- after the player crossed a seam, or as a
save loaded -- fell through absent collision, which reads as air, and never
stopped.  Now [[lisp:luft.terrain:scene-collision-resident-p]] is the
freeze test: every body in a =world-simulation= (players and companions
alike), turtles, and the viewer's player wait where it is false.  The
teleport of #OLWSWF was the same gap: the destination was visible but not
collided with.  The sweep was not at fault; a body dropped on ground at
-50 to -50000 cells/s lands on it.

/Stopgap./ =edit-streaming-scene-cell= now reads occupancy only from the
resident chunk, which every visible chunk has, and keeps writing collision
only inside its window.  It stays: a guest keeps collision only around
itself, yet sees and receives events for chunks farther away.  A terrain
test reproduces the phase-1 crash against the code before it.

Tests: collision around two distant foci while publication follows one;
turtles and gnomes wait on uncollided ground and stand once it collides
(fails without the fix); and a loopback guest five chunks from the host,
beside whom a body walks on the host's ground.

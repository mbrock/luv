#+title: Frame slots as a luv case study
#+startup: overview

* The frame-slot episode
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: R3V8HC
:END:

On 8 August 2026, commit
[[https://github.com/mbrock/luv/commit/dfbf3fc05a71f2225e79dd58962cd7f63b48e670][=dfbf3fc=]] replaced a queue-wide idle wait in Vulkan canvas presentation with
two rotating frame slots and completion fences.  The immediate problem was
allowing CPU recording of a new presentation frame to overlap execution of an
earlier one.

Before that change, =vkQueueWaitIdle= did more than slow the loop down.  It was
also the proof that command pools, command buffers, framebuffers, staging
resources, and related native objects from the submission were no longer in
use.  Removing the wait without replacing that proof would have made their
destruction or reuse unsafe.

The slot change became more intelligible after studying the broader lifetime
model #L8R2WF: asynchronous submission creates an interval during which the
application has moved on but the implementation must retain everything the GPU
may still touch.  That understanding arrived after the practical change,
which is exactly the kind of event this wiki should help make less accidental.

This is a retrospective interpretation, not evidence that the current design
is ideal.

* What a current luv frame slot means
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: F7Q2KM
:END:

In the current canvas Vulkan path, each of two slots owns:

- a fence signaled by its render submission;
- an image-acquisition semaphore;
- the submitted =gpu-command-buffer= and therefore its native command
  resources.

Before reusing a slot, luv waits for that slot's fence.  Only then does it
destroy the previous command-buffer resources and reset the fence.  The
submitted commands are transferred into the slot after submission, so an
=unwind-protect= in the calling frame does not destroy them prematurely.

Render-finished semaphores are indexed separately by acquired swapchain image.
This is a reminder that “frame,” “CPU frame slot,” “queue submission,” and
“swapchain image” are overlapping but non-identical lifetimes.

The relevant current definitions are
[[file:../canvas-vulkan.lisp::(defclass vulkan-canvas-frame-slot][=vulkan-canvas-frame-slot=]],
[[file:../canvas-vulkan.lisp::(defun recycle-vulkan-canvas-frame-slot][=recycle-vulkan-canvas-frame-slot=]], and
[[file:../canvas-vulkan.lisp::(defun call-with-vulkan-canvas-frame][=call-with-vulkan-canvas-frame=]].

* What the slots do not establish
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: N5D9AX
:END:

The two slots establish a bounded reuse protocol for one presentation path.
They do not yet constitute a general luv resource-lifetime system.

In particular, they do not by themselves answer:

- how an arbitrary buffer destroyed after submission remains physically alive;
- how non-presentation compute or copy submissions are tracked;
- whether several submissions belonging to one conceptual frame share a
  retirement point;
- how =mapAsync=-like host access would wait only for uses of one buffer;
- whether completed resources are reclaimed without waiting to revisit a
  particular frame slot;
- how device loss retires or abandons in-flight ownership.

Current public =submit= remains synchronous and waits for queue idle, so it
sidesteps many of these questions at a substantial concurrency cost.  The
canvas path is a special asynchronous path.  Both facts are provisional.

This is why frame slots must not be confused with completion-frontier
reclamation #P6Q4MT or described as a WebGPU frame-end mechanism #E7N3KP.

* Questions exposed by the experiment
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: J4C6WP
:END:

The experiment gives us better questions for the next design round:

- Should luv have a general submission object or monotonically increasing
  submission index?
- Should a submitted command buffer transfer ownership into the queue until a
  completion frontier passes it?
- Can presentation frame slots become clients of that general mechanism rather
  than a second lifetime system?
- Which resources should be pooled and reset after completion, and which
  should simply be dropped?
- Should ordinary Lisp =destroy= mean logical invalidation with physical
  destruction deferred automatically?
- How much of this remains invisible behind a deliberately small synchronous
  API, and what useful asynchronous operations should be public?

The wgpu mechanism #G5X8BN is one worked example.  It is valuable because it
makes the ownership transfers concrete, not because luv ought to reproduce its
types or complexity.

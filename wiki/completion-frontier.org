#+title: The queue frontier and modern-capabilities decision
#+startup: overview

* Luv assumes modern Vulkan
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: Q2W7HD
:END:

On 11 August 2026 luv raised its requested Vulkan API version to 1.4 and
adopted timeline semaphores and synchronization2 as the only synchronization
vocabulary it speaks.  The legacy alternatives were removed rather than kept
as fallbacks: the =VkFence= vocabulary, =vkQueueSubmit=, and the parallel
wait-stage arrays of =VkSubmitInfo= are gone from the owned binding.

The reasoning is a stance, not an accident: luv assumes modern capabilities
until there is a reason not to.  Timeline semaphores are mandatory core
since Vulkan 1.2 and synchronization2 since 1.3, so on any 1.3+ device there
is no conformant configuration in which the fallback would run.  Carrying a
fence-pool tier would be legacy-support complexity with no user.  The local
development environment (MoltenVK 1.4.1 via nix, advertising Vulkan 1.4.350
at instance and device level) was verified before committing to this.

Two consequences worth remembering:

- Device creation now chains
  =VkPhysicalDeviceTimelineSemaphoreFeatures= and
  =VkPhysicalDeviceSynchronization2Features= and will fail loudly on a
  driver that cannot honor them.  There is deliberately no negotiation.
- The 1.4 demand also makes dynamic rendering and host image copies
  available unconditionally.  Neither is used yet; both could delete
  machinery (the render-pass/framebuffer cache, the texture-upload staging
  path) in later rounds.

* One queue, one timeline, one frontier
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: T9K4RC
:END:

The Vulkan GPU queue now owns a timeline semaphore and a monotonically
increasing submission counter.  Every submission passes through
=vkQueueSubmit2= and signals the timeline with a fresh index; the semaphore
counter read back from the device *is* the completion frontier of #P6Q4MT.
Public =submit= still blocks, but it now waits for its own submission index
rather than calling =vkQueueWaitIdle=: the proof shrank from "the whole
queue is idle" to "the prefix ending at my submission is complete," which
no longer serializes against unrelated in-flight work such as canvas
frames.

Each submission also pushes a record — index, the submitted command
buffers, and every resource they captured — onto a queue-owned list of
live submissions.  A maintenance step runs after each submit and after
explicit waits, retiring every record the frontier has passed.  A
recursive mutex on the queue guards the counter, the records, and the
scheduled texture layouts, since the SDL canvas thread and the REPL both
submit.

[[file:../gpu/vulkan.lisp::(defun vulkan-queue-completed-frontier][=vulkan-queue-completed-frontier=]],
[[file:../gpu/vulkan.lisp::(defun maintain-vulkan-queue][=maintain-vulkan-queue=]],
[[file:../gpu/vulkan.lisp::(defun wait-for-vulkan-submission][=wait-for-vulkan-submission=]], and
[[file:../gpu/vulkan.lisp::(defun submit-vulkan-command-buffers][=submit-vulkan-command-buffers=]].

* Commands capture their dependencies; destroy defers through them
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: K8W2FN
:END:

Encoding now retains, in an encoder-owned set, every wrapper a recorded
command depends on: pipelines, bind groups and their views, samplers, and
uniform buffers, alongside the tracked textures.  =finish= transfers the
set into the command buffer, and submission stamps each member with the
submission index as its last use.  This closes the missing ownership edge
of #B2M7QX for the current command vocabulary.

On that foundation, =destroy= finally means what #D3H7VK describes.  The
public operation marks the wrapper destroyed immediately — no new work can
mention it — and then either performs the native teardown at once, when
the frontier has already passed the object's last use, or attaches the
teardown to the still-live submission record that used it last.  Retiring
that record performs the deferred destruction.  Device destruction waits
for idle and drains every record, so nothing is leaked or torn down early.
=define-vulkan-resource-destroy= in
[[file:../gpu/vulkan.lisp::(defmacro define-vulkan-resource-destroy][=gpu/vulkan.lisp=]] gives every resource class the same split.

The payoff is the contract change this whole page exists for: public
=submit= is now asynchronous.  It returns a submission index as soon as
the work is scheduled, and destroying any resource the batch used —
including the command buffer itself — is safe immediately, exactly the
WebGPU shape.  =submitted-work-done= is the explicit, publicly exported
completion wait, the synchronous analog of
=GPUQueue.onSubmittedWorkDone()=.  The queue texture-write convenience is
asynchronous for the same reason; its staging buffer rides the private
command buffer through the frontier.  The demos' teardown paths, which
destroy pipelines and bind groups while their last presented frame is
still in flight, exercise this for real.

* Frame slots became frontier clients
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: M6P8XW
:END:

The canvas frame slots of #F7Q2KM no longer own fences.  A slot records the
submission index of its frame; recycling waits for the queue frontier to
reach that index and then destroys the retained command buffer.  The
image-acquisition and render-done binary semaphores remain, because
swapchain acquisition and presentation still speak binary semaphores —
presentation pacing and lifetime proof are different jobs, and only the
second one moved to the frontier.

Now that destruction defers through the frontier on its own, the slot's
wait is no longer a safety requirement at all: it is purely the pacing
policy that bounds CPU frames in flight to the slot count.

This answers one question from #J4C6WP as hoped: presentation slots are now
clients of the general mechanism rather than a second lifetime system.

* Leaked references are a warned failure, not a management strategy
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: X7J4TB
:END:

Explicit =destroy= is luv's resource contract.  The garbage collector is
deliberately not the lifetime system: it is the tripwire that tells you
the contract was broken.  Every Vulkan wrapper (and each recording
command encoder) installs a finalizer at creation; explicit destruction
cancels it silently, while a wrapper reclaimed by the collector signals a
=gpu-resource-leaked= warning naming the class and label, records the
condition in =*leaked-gpu-resources*= for REPL auditing, and then frees
the native resources anyway so a long live session does not bleed GPU
memory while you ignore the warnings.

Three structural facts make the safety net sound:

- A finalizer closure must not capture the wrapper it guards, or the
  wrapper never becomes garbage.  =define-vulkan-resource-destroy=
  therefore extracts the native handles into the closure up front, and
  the same closure serves as both =destroy-vulkan-native= and the leak
  finalizer.
- Because each closure captures the device *wrapper*, a device stays
  reachable until every collected child's pending finalizer has run:
  child-before-device finalization ordering falls out of reachability
  rather than needing a registry.
- Submission records hold their resources strongly, so a wrapper can
  only be collected after the frontier has passed its last use.  A
  finalizer's teardown is therefore always immediately safe; it never
  needs to defer.  Teardown on the finalizer thread is serialized with
  submission and device destruction by the queue lock.

Two honest limitations: the warning is eventual, not prompt, since a
stale conservative stack reference can delay collection past several
GCs; and a device that is itself dropped cannot reclaim native teardown
that had been deferred onto its queue's records, because those records
became garbage with it — the leaked-device warning still fires.

* What the frontier does not yet establish
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: V3N7QJ
:END:

The ownership gaps of #S8R5JV are now closed for the current command
vocabulary: capture is transitive, destruction defers, submission is
asynchronous, the queue state is locked, and dropped references are
warned about and reclaimed (#X7J4TB).  What remains open is adjacent
rather than internal:

- Nothing maps or reads back yet, so the frontier has no =mapAsync=-like
  client, and =write-buffer= on a persistently mapped uniform buffer is
  still excluded from GPU reads only by the compositor's slot pacing
  (#B9Q4LC).
- The capture set is defined by the encode methods that exist.  A new
  command kind must remember to retain what it references; there is no
  completeness check connecting command types to retention.
- Access-hazard tracking is still the narrow layout model of #X2D7MF;
  the frontier proves lifetime, not the absence of missing barriers.
- Retirement happens at submits and explicit waits.  A long-idle queue
  holds its last record and any deferred teardown until the next
  maintenance point; nothing polls in the background.

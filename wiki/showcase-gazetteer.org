#+title: The luvcraft showcase gazetteer
#+startup: overview

* The showcase is an atlas with three lanes
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: RFUR2R
:END:

The showcase should say what luvcraft can actually do, preserve good views of
those things, and occasionally be beautiful enough to stand on its own.  Those
are related jobs, but not identical ones.  The useful catalogue has three
lanes:

- *Scenes* are composed editorial images: terrain, inhabitants, architecture,
  and light arranged as a picture.  They normally omit the crosshair, hotbar,
  and first-person body.
- *Surfaces* show interaction in context: a terminal wall, phone, inventory,
  metabar, approval, or other pane at the moment its purpose is legible.  HUD
  and viewmodel layers belong when they are part of that purpose.
- *Plates* hold a camera and one variable still for comparison: materials,
  hours, shadows, blocklight, glyphs, poses, and backend-sensitive effects.
  These are reference imagery even when they are too diagnostic for the front
  of the public page.

This page is a source-backed roster, not a claim that all these recipes exist.
Names in =verbatim= are proposed literal capture names.  Each figure can own a
still and one or more films under the executable media contract #IVRWI8.  A
recipe only joins =luv/showcase= when it can render from source without private
ambient state; networked, credentialed, and Darwin-only views remain authored
shots until they acquire a deterministic fixture.

The first folio should be small enough to art-direct: the pre-noon ridge
#NLCFX0, a crystal afterglow #Q8ZIFD, a terminal at home in the terrain
#2TMUKK, the instrument family #DYJZBK, inhabitants #W7T2IT, physics
#CSCFGF, and the already composed birthday world #L5G2WQ.  The rest of the
gazetteer prevents those hero shots from becoming the only visual memory of
the game.

* Pre-noon light on the little world
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: NLCFX0
:END:

The principal landscape should be a recognizably generated little world, not a
bespoke diorama.  [[file:../luvcraft/terrain.lisp][The terrain source]] already
supplies rolling grass, sand, stone and snow with rocks and broad-canopied
trees.  [[file:../luvcraft/sky.lisp][The sky]] can pin any hour, while the HDR
presentation in [[file:../luvcraft/render.lisp][the renderer]] supplies fog,
bloom, vignette, focus blur, and screen-space sunlight shafts.

The composition wants a ridge or shallow valley from seed 121 or another
recorded seed: warm light from the left at roughly ten in the morning, the sun
just inside or just outside the frame, two or three tree crowns breaking it
into shafts, a bright middle-distance slope, and a cooler fogged horizon.  The
camera should be near human height rather than the old gazetteer's aerial
debugging angle.  The grade may make the pre-noon sun warmer, but should not
raise shaft or bloom gain until the entire frame looks veiled.

- =pre-noon-ridge-still= — clean 3:2 hero still, no HUD or viewmodel.
- =pre-noon-ridge-glide= — six to eight seconds moving slowly across the
  foreground while keeping the ridge and sun relationship intact.
- =pre-noon-under-the-trees= — a lower companion view with trunks and leaves
  providing foreground occlusion and obvious shafts.

The still establishes the picture.  The glide proves that shadows, fog, lens
effects, streamed terrain products, and exposure remain coherent when the
camera moves.

* The sky deserves one fixed camera across a whole day
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: TRYHPN
:END:

A time-of-day folio should use one seed, one camera, one viewport, and one
grade.  Only the pinned =sky-clock= reading changes.  That makes the default
profile's midnight, sunrise, morning, noon, sunset, and dusk keyframes visible
as a system rather than as unrelated pretty pictures.

- =little-world-blue-hour= — the world still readable before sunrise.
- =little-world-sunrise= — warm horizon, long shadows, cool zenith.
- =little-world-pre-noon= — the neutral comparison for #NLCFX0.
- =little-world-noon= — high sun, shortest shadows, strongest material read.
- =little-world-sunset= — warm lateral light and fog colour.
- =little-world-deep-night= — ambient silhouette without an artificial moon.
- =little-world-one-day= — an eight-second authored time-lapse through the same
  fixed view; no camera orbit to confuse lighting change with parallax.

The six stills are reference plates.  The film is where continuous profile
interpolation, shadow motion, cloud movement, and exposure transitions become
visible.

* Crystal afterglow shows luminous materials
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: Q8ZIFD
:END:

The crystal is simultaneously a textured material, a blocklight emitter, and
an HDR surface emitter.  A small grove at late dusk can show all three: crystal
light reaching grass, stone, trunks, and the underside of leaves; faceted
normal relief on the near blocks; and a restrained bloom spread against a dark
sky.  The existing =glow-floor= and =crystal-seam= gazetteer views already
provide the diagnostic core in
[[file:../luvcraft/gazetteer.lisp][luvcraft/gazetteer.lisp]].

- =crystal-grove-afterglow-still= — a composed near/middle/far scene with one
  crystal close enough to read its facets and several hidden among trees.
- =crystal-grove-night-orbit= — a slow lateral arc, not a full turntable, so
  changing occlusion shows the blocklight volume.
- =crystal-chunk-seam-plate= — the existing flat seam proof promoted into the
  capture system as reference imagery.
- =crystal-bloom-ladder= — three fixed plates at zero, ordinary, and strong
  bloom gain for tuning regressions; never a public hero card.

* A terminal is a place, a pane, and a carried thing
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 2TMUKK
:END:

[[file:../luvcraft/terminal-wall.lisp][The world terminal]] discovers an authored
rectangle of blocks, sizes a Ghostty grid to it, draws styled cell backgrounds
and Slug glyph geometry, and adds a separately lit screen panel and glass
faceplate.  The same terminal surface protocol reaches the graphite slab in
[[file:../luvcraft/phone.lisp][the player's hand]].  A terminal showcase should
therefore keep enough oblique perspective for the rim, glass, and world
placement to survive; a square-on crop makes the surface look like a pasted
screenshot.

- =terminal-camp-golden-hour= — a three-by-two or four-by-three shell wall in a
  small stone-and-wood shelter, warm exterior light, luminous styled text, and
  no modal HUD.
- =terminal-shell-closeup= — the same deterministic fixture from its ordinary
  off-axis focus pose, sharp enough to inspect bold and coloured cells.
- =terminal-mode-hotbar= — focused wall plus the mode-aware hotbar showing
  Shell, Film, and Telegram as the wall's own choices.
- =phone-shell-in-hand= — first-person body and phone retained, terminal full
  of a short deterministic fixture rather than a machine-specific prompt.
- =phone-focus-pull= — a short film of taking the phone out, glancing at it,
  focusing it, typing one line, leaving focus, and pocketing it while the shell
  remains alive.

The shell fixture should contain colour, bold text, a command and output, but
no hostname, home directory, credential, or wall-clock reading.

* Player instruments should be readable in context
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: DYJZBK
:END:

The main panes are already McCLIM frames composited into the one game canvas.
They should be photographed over a quiet midtone landscape which gives their
edges contrast without competing with their text.

- =hotbar-material-palette= — the ordinary bottom hotbar with procedural block
  icons, numbered wells, selection edge, and selected-material caption from
  [[file:../mcclim/hotbar.lisp][mcclim/hotbar.lisp]].
- =inventory-natural=, =inventory-building=, and =inventory-luminous= — the
  category sidebar, paged item grid, quickbar, quantity, and item inspector
  from [[file:../mcclim/inventory.lisp][mcclim/inventory.lisp]].  The building
  plate should inspect a film so its title/uploader/duration specialization is
  retained in visual memory.
- =metabar-grading= — the grading group open over the #NLCFX0 scene, so
  exposure, bloom, light-shaft, shaft-reach, and vignette controls are visibly
  attached to their consequence.
- =metabar-physics= — the physics group over a sleeping pile of balls; useful
  as a reference for the knob/action vocabulary rather than a hero shot.
- =keymap-legend= — the generated sections and live bindings from
  [[file:../luvcraft/clim/legend.lisp][the keymap legend]].
- =command-menu-filter= — =M-x= with a useful partial query and several matches,
  showing that game commands are an inspectable command table.
- =tape-prompt= — the small YouTube-code prompt with neutral fixture text and
  its validation line.
- =lobby-radio= — the quiet corner instrument in offline, connecting, and
  two-peer fixture states; only the two-peer plate belongs on the public page.

An authored =instrument-tour= film may open hotbar, inventory, metabar, legend,
and command menu in a stable sequence, but the individual stills remain the
better reference for typography and layout.

* World-native panes should keep their depth cues
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: FSDUXL
:END:

Some panes live on world surfaces rather than in the HUD.  Their showcase
pair is an environmental view proving placement and a close view proving
content.  Both keep the surface slightly off-axis.

- =film-browser-on-wall= — the stone-cased file browser from
  [[file:../mcclim/terminal-film-browser.lisp][terminal-film-browser.lisp]],
  with directories, playable films, sizes, paging marks, and footer message
  supplied by a temporary deterministic fixture tree.
- =communicator-dialogs-on-wall= — a credential-free Telegram console snapshot
  containing avatar colours, dialog rows, transcript text, photo/video
  thumbnails, composer, and scrollbar.  A live private account must never be
  a capture dependency.
- =paper-sheet-on-wall= — the separate =luvcraft/paper= system showing a known
  repository PDF page with McCLIM text replayed as sharp world geometry.
- =terminal-six-faces= — one compact reference scene exercising front, back,
  left, right, top, and bottom terminal surface frames.

The communicator and paper are real capabilities, but need small source-owned
fixtures before they can enter the default Chapel render set.

* Inhabitants need portraits and motion
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: W7T2IT
:END:

The little world now has box-built turtles and independently ray-marched gnome
and cat bodies.  Their pictures should show scale against blocks and ground,
not isolate them on a transparent turntable.

- =turtle-meadow-portrait= — promote the three posed animals from the old
  gazetteer into a warmer, lower composition with walking, turning, and resting
  poses all readable.
- =turtle-crossing= — a short lateral film of gait, rest, turning, cast shadow,
  and a small terrain obstacle.
- =ride-a-turtle= — first-person authored film of mounting with Tab, walking a
  small path from the moving seat, and dismounting safely.
- =gnome-garden-portrait= — one gnome at conversational distance among grass,
  stone and flowers, preserving hat, beard, face, rim light, and world scale.
- =cat-in-the-sun= — the seated cat in warm side light with the tail silhouette
  clear against a simpler background.
- =gnome-and-cat-two-shot= — the shared embodied-agent vocabulary made visible
  without pretending the two bodies share a shader.
- =inhabitant-turns= — a restrained film of turtle gait, gnome walking, and the
  cat's breathing proxy, cut as three shots rather than one busy arena.

* Agent work has four visible surfaces
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: HA4V5Q
:END:

The agent is visually larger than its gnome.  Its work appears as a top-right
cassette HUD, bubbles and dialogue around an embodied presence, an ANSI
transcript written onto a terminal wall, and a clean over-the-shoulder
surroundings observation.  Construction adds the fifth special case already
captured by #Y7X7WK: an inert possible world awaiting approval.

- =agent-cassette-hud= — a source-owned synthetic turn with prompt, thought,
  one running call, one completed call, elapsed times, output, and final text.
  Do not call a model to render it.
- =gnome-conversation= — the same synthetic records as world bubbles plus a
  short dialogue subtitle, with the gnome itself in frame.
- =agent-wall-transcript= — the records rendered as coloured Ghostty geometry
  on a terminal wall, including prompt and one tool cassette.
- =agent-surroundings-glance= — the clean third-person image from
  =capture-surroundings-observation=, paired in the wiki with its pose and
  voxel-census text.
- =construction-proposal-still= and =construction-proposal-orbit= — retain the
  existing recipes; add a future =construction-approve= film which ends with
  the preview becoming ordinary world geometry.

Synthetic transcript objects keep typography and layout deterministic.  A
separate authored film may show a real model turn, but it should be presented
as an observed session rather than as a reproducible capture recipe.

* Physics is best shown with causality
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: CSCFGF
:END:

[[file:../luvcraft/balls.lisp][The physics presentation]] draws true patterned
spheres, posts the player and animals as kinematic obstacles, and turns the
fountain and lava spring blocks into persistent body sources.  A still can
show materials and a settled pile; motion is required to show the system.

- =ball-in-hand= — viewmodel reference with the worn banded ball carried low.
- =throw-and-settle= — one throw, one bounce, a roll down a small slope, contact
  with another ball, and sleep; keep the camera fixed so causality reads.
- =party-ball-cascade= — beach, smiley, marble, and classic balls spilling down
  stone steps under side light.
- =turtle-and-ball= — a rolling ball deflected by a turtle, which visibly
  startles; a playful proof that physics and critter protocols meet.
- =fountain-courtyard= — water drops spraying from the fountain block and
  splashing over a built basin.
- =lava-spring-night= — glowing gobbets rising, falling, sticking, cooling, and
  fading against a dark slate setting.
- =dig-and-place-particles= — a close short film of one removal and placement,
  preserving the ordinary block-particle feedback.

The spring films need a bounded clock and fixed emission sequence so their
authored motion remains repeatable enough to compare.

* The birthday world is the authored ensemble shot
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: L5G2WQ
:END:

The =luvcraft/birthday= system is already a composed scene rather than a bag
of demonstrations.  [[file:../luvcraft/birthday/party.lisp][The party owner]]
places a gazebo in a flowered meadow, ties back-lit balloons to it, rings it
with dancing gnomes and physical balls, runs a terminal-based marquee, pins
the sky, and starts the fireworks.  It deserves both a coherent film and
smaller portraits of the work that disappears in the ensemble.

- =birthday-gazebo-golden-hour= — wide establishing still with meadow, flower
  ring, gazebo, marquee, balloons, gnome troupe, and enough terrain beyond to
  locate the party in a world.
- =birthday-balloon-backlight= — close still with latex glint, transmitted
  sunlight, strings, gazebo edge, and one dancing gnome for scale.
- =birthday-marquee-and-gnomes= — medium still preserving animated banner
  design and the troupe's individual colours.
- =birthday-fireworks= — the existing staged party film or a six-to-ten-second
  extract containing launch, peony, ring, and willow shapes over the gazebo.
- =birthday-afterparty= — turtles entering after the main act among settled
  party balls.
- =birthday-cutscenes= — the complete authored two-act film remains a feature,
  not a default looping card if its length overwhelms the rest of the page.

* A world within a world needs its own folio
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: TWKA93
:END:

Film walls and portals both put a moving image onto a world panel, but they
prove different things.  Film uses the hardware-backed video path and can be
captured on the showcase renderer with a tiny generated clip.  A portal shows
another live luvcraft process through a shared IOSurface and is Darwin-only.

- =wall-cinema-at-dusk= — a deterministic locally generated film playing on a
  terminal rectangle while the faceplate, shelter, ground, and dusk light
  remain visible.
- =choose-and-play-film= — authored sequence from Film mode, through the wall
  browser, to moving picture and back to Shell.
- =film-reel-in-inventory= — the carried film's title, uploader, duration, and
  flange icon in the item inspector.
- =terminal-portal= — a child little world framed by its parent's terminal
  wall, with enough oblique perspective to show it is a transmission surface.
- =portal-recursion= — an authored Darwin film moving the child camera while
  the parent moves slightly too; the two parallax systems should remain
  distinguishable and tear-free.

Portal entries stay out of the Chapel default set unless the render host and
capture operation acquire an explicit Darwin route.  “Hw accelerated” film
captures require the hardware path; a silent software fallback does not prove
the feature being shown.

* Reference plates make failures legible
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: OK1OC8
:END:

The diagnostic gazetteer remains valuable beside the editorial showcase.  Its
existing views should be wrapped by small capture recipes instead of copied or
retired: =gazetteer-little-world-noon=, =little-world-dusk=, =shadow-forest=,
=glow-floor=, =crystal-seam=, =turtle-meadow=, and =shadow-yard=.

The broader reference shelf should add:

- =material-raking-lineup= — every placeable block in category order under one
  low neutral sun, including colour and generated normal relief.
- =atlas-colour-and-normal= — exact colour and normal atlases as technical
  plates, not upscaled screenshots of blocks.
- =shadow-yard-still= and =shadow-yard-motion= — simple caster/receiver evidence
  plus a consecutive camera or sun sequence.
- =shadow-forest-motion= — representative trees and terrain paired with the
  existing derivative diagnostics, keeping host timing out of a visual claim.
- =world-text-closeup= — the existing Slug text proof at native resolution.
- =terminal-type-plate= — regular/bold, ANSI colours, background cells, cursor,
  and faceplate at near and far camera distances.
- =body-proportion-plate= — gnome and cat at one block ruler for shader-knob
  reviews.
- =viewport-shapes= — the same quiet scene at landscape, square, portrait, and
  a resized live viewport, preserving safe HUD insets.

Reference captures should keep their ordinary PNGs and short MP4s available
even when only selected scenes appear on the front showcase grid.  The current
flat manifest does not need categories yet: the page can remain flat until
enough real captures make navigation a demonstrated problem.

* Every still has a motion companion when time matters
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: I380Q9
:END:

An orbit is useful when the claim is shape, depth, or attachment to the world.
It is not a universal video template.  The film should move the variable that
the subject owns:

| Subject | Still establishes | Film must establish |
|---------+-------------------+---------------------|
| Terrain light | Composition and grade | Stable shafts, shadows, fog, streaming |
| Day cycle | Keyframe appearances | Continuous interpolation and shadow motion |
| Terminal | Glyph and surface quality | Focus, typing, mode and lifetime transitions |
| Pane | Layout and legibility | Opening, selection, feedback and dismissal |
| Inhabitant | Model, material and scale | Gait, breath, turn, ride or conversation |
| Physics | Material and settled state | Contact, bounce, flow, cooling and sleep |
| Proposal | Finished counterfactual | View-dependent orbit and approval transition |
| Portal or film | Panel placement | Independent moving image without tearing |

[[lisp:capture-luvcraft-screenshot]] already chooses HUD and viewmodel layers,
and [[lisp:film-luvcraft-session]] gives authored code a =before-frame=
boundary.  A recipe should reuse one scene constructor across its still and
film whenever they depict the same subject.  That makes their difference an
authored camera or clock path rather than accidental world state.

* DONE Open the phone-first gallery
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: GS5XNW
:END:

/Intent./ Grow the first folio into something that can be browsed for pleasure:
dozens of distinct stills and films, with clean portrait compositions taking
the lead on phones and a smaller set of landscape play views retaining the
crosshair, hotbar, and first-person body when those are the subject.

/Evidence./ Source revision =a3283ebe9a0a= publishes 39 capture recipes: 27
stills and 12 films, including 14 portrait cards.  The portrait films use the
LUFT Atelier vocabulary from #SY26PO: a 720 by 1280 frame, authored
position/look/FOV paths, eased Catmull-Rom travel, field scale 1.25, collision
clearance, and a clean presentation.  New terrain, day-cycle, crystal,
inhabitant, and physics subjects sit beside one ordinary landscape walk with
its HUD and viewmodel.

The public index serves 480-pixel WebP stills and video posters, with
768-pixel WebP alternatives for landscape stills; the PNG and MP4 originals
remain linked.  At a 390 by 844 browser viewport, all 39 phone-card assets
total 503,406 bytes.  The 12 videos remain at media =readyState= zero until the
visitor plays one.  Browser acceptance played the LUFT clay flight, crystal
night arc, party-ball cascade, and landscape HUD walk; each decoded at its
declared 720 by 1280 or 1200 by 800 dimensions and advanced continuously.

Chapel's RX 6600 XT rendered two raw Vulkan jobs in 10.195 seconds in parallel
versus 20.276 seconds sequentially, with byte-identical PNG frames.  Parallel
x264 did not pass the stronger film gate: encoded files and decoded frames
differed from an isolated render even with one encoder thread.  The published
path therefore remains sequential and now fails closed unless Vulkan resolves
the explicit RADV ICD to the discrete RX 6600 XT.

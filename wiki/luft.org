#+title: LUFT: packed sites, chains, and the atelier
#+startup: overview

LUFT is a small complement to luv: a way of naming the vertices, edges, faces,
and cells of a cubical world with one fixnum each, and of treating a block
world as algebra rather than as voxels with decorations.  Its first client
is a greenfield renderer, an atelier free of the luvcraft mesher, that draws
a textureless block world from shaders reading those fixnums straight from a
GPU buffer -- by vertex pulling on any device, or by task and mesh shaders
where those run.  This page records the vocabulary, the algebra that makes
the surface fall out, the shader-language extensions the GPU path needed,
and the shape of the pipeline.

* A site is a lattice anchor, an extent mask, and a polarity
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: KE4P5F
:END:

A /site/ is a 60-bit unsigned value: three extent bits =XYZ= and one polarity
bit in the low positions, then a 24-bit X anchor, a 24-bit Y anchor, and an
8-bit Z anchor.  Each extent bit says the site spans the unit interval forward
from its anchor along that axis, so no extent is a vertex, one is an edge, two
a face, and three a cell.  The clear polarity bit is positive and the set bit
negative; [[lisp:luft:opposite-site]] toggles it without changing the site's
geometry.

Incidence is canonical: the high X boundary of the cell at =(x,y,z)= has the
same geometry as the low X boundary of the cell at =(x+1,y,z)=, but the two
faces have opposite polarities.  X and Y wrap with the period a
[[lisp:luft:world-domain]] chooses; Z is 256 non-wrapping planes.

The oriented boundary follows from the ordered axes in the extent: the high
boundary along the =i=-th present axis has the canonical polarity =(-1)^i=,
the low boundary has the opposite polarity, and a negative input site reverses
every result.  Thus [[lisp:luft:map-site-boundary]] returns signed sites
directly, and =∂∂ = 0= is an executable annihilation test in
=luft/tests.lisp=.

* A block world is a 3-chain and its surface is a boundary
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 9HLYEE
:END:

A [[lisp:luft:chain]] is a collection of signed sites.  Its implementation is
a pair of hash-table ledgers, one per polarity, both keyed by geometry with the
polarity bit masked away.  Adding a site annihilates one opposite site already
in the other ledger.  Private occurrence counts make that result independent
of arrival order; an ordinary solid and its surface still contain at most one
copy of any signed site.

A solid world is the 3-chain containing one positive site for every occupied
cell.  Its visible surface is not a meshing problem but an algebraic fact:
[[lisp:luft:boundary-chain]] applies #KE4P5F's oriented boundary to every
site.  Two solid neighbours send their shared face to opposite polarity
ledgers, so the pair annihilates; only exposed signed faces survive.  A face's
polarity reverses or preserves its canonical orientation to give its outward
normal, and =∂(∂ solid) = 0= says the surface is closed.  Editing a cell later
means adding its signed boundary or the opposite boundary, not remeshing
anything.

The luvcraft mesher walks chunk fields on the CPU and emits atlas-textured
quads (see [[file:block-world.org][the block world]]); LUFT's
[[lisp:luft:surface-chain]] is a pair of polarity ledgers holding signed face
sites.  Both are right for their worlds; the second is what a mesh shader
wants.

* The GPU reads signed sites directly
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: AGVXGM
:END:

A surface face is already one signed 60-bit site, with no separate wrapper or
coefficient encoding.  It remains an immediate fixnum on 64-bit SBCL, and a
specialized vector of sites can be copied directly into the
=(unsigned-byte 64)= buffer the shader reads; no vertex is ever built on the
CPU.  The renderer orders the sites by eight-cell chunk, pads them to whole
/bricks/ of [[lisp:luft.render.shaders:+brick-size+]] sites, and measures one
bounding sphere per brick as four floats —
two arrays and one uniform block are the whole upload.

The four buffer bits above the site are deliberately unearned.  A per-face
material would fit there, but the occurrence is still a chain site, and
materials belong to cells; the atelier is textureless on purpose until that
question is asked properly.

* Storage buffers and bit fields enter the shader language
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: HFX2LI
:END:

The mathematical shader language of #S4H8DR had textures, samplers, and a
vec4-only uniform block, and no way to read an array of 64-bit words.  Two
additions carry a site into a shader:

- A =:storage-buffer= resource with an =:element= type, indexed by
  =buffer-element=; the array has no declared length, and a bare reference to
  the buffer is an error.  Metal lowers it to =const device T* name
  [[buffer(n)]]= bound through the same argument-table address slot as a
  uniform buffer, and SPIR-V to a =StorageBuffer= block holding one
  =RuntimeArray= with an explicit stride, asking for
  =SPV_KHR_storage_buffer_storage_class= in 1.0 modules.
- =ldb= with a literal =(byte size position)= on a =:uint= or =:uint64=
  value, so a shader unpacks a site with the very words =luft.lisp= uses.
  Metal lowers it to a shift and a mask in the operand's width; SPIR-V to
  two logical shifts, because Vulkan restricts bit-field instructions to
  32-bit operands and the language has no 64-bit constants.

Both are raw representations until interpreted: they carry no quantity, so an
unannotated graph stays valid legacy source and an annotated one still
refuses to mix.  The Metal HAL gained the matching =:storage= buffer usage,
=:storage-buffer= binding type, typed =write-buffer= for unsigned arrays,
and depth-stencil state on mesh pipelines.

* Task workgroups cull bricks; mesh lanes unpack faces
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: VAABY9
:END:

[[file:images/luft-atelier.png]]

The pipeline in =luft/render/shaders.lisp= has three stages and no vertex
buffer.  A task workgroup owns one brick: it reads the brick's sphere,
tests it against the view pyramid with a workgroup-uniform expression, and
emits one mesh workgroup or none, handing the brick index through a
one-word payload.  Each mesh lane then loads one site with =buffer-element=,
extracts anchor, extent, and polarity with =ldb=, and writes four vertices and
two triangles.  The extent names the face's two spanning axes in canonical
X<Y<Z order, their cross product is the canonical orientation, and the
polarity times that orientation is the outward normal — the boundary
operator's orientation rendered directly.  A lane whose site is zero, or
whose face turns away from the camera by the exact plane test, scales its quad
to a point instead of branching on output counts, which the language requires
to be uniform.

The rounded and chamfered mesh makes the brick grain a hardware contract, not
just a batching choice.  One face's 6×6 point grid emits 36 vertices and 50
triangles, so a five-face workgroup declares 180 vertices and 250 primitives,
inside =VK_EXT_mesh_shader='s guaranteed minima of 256 for each.  The earlier
seven-face brick declared only 252 vertices but 350 primitives; RADV accepted
the pipeline and then reset the GPU on its first submission.  The generated
SPIR-V and a host test now state both halves of the budget.

The fragment stage is textureless by design: a top, side, and bottom tone,
a soft line where faces meet, sun and ambient light, and fog toward the sky.
The whole atelier is =luft/render=: a scene holds the solid chain and its
products, a renderer holds the GPU objects, [[lisp:luft.render:render-to-png]]
draws headlessly, and [[lisp:luft.render:start-viewer]] opens a window with a
fly camera.  Its test turns the camera straight up and expects only sky,
which is the frustum test doing real work.

The standalone atelier creates only the flat surface pipeline by default.
=LUFT_RENDER_MODE=clear= submits a clear and copy with no shader pipeline;
=sky= adds only the background; =flat=, =bevel=, =chamfer=, and =paper= each
create only their named surface pipeline; =full= restores every style plus the
sky and lens.  Thus a driver failure can be localized by increasing one
semantic layer at a time rather than constructing and dispatching the entire
atelier before the first frame.  =LUFT_RENDER_TECHNIQUE=mesh= asks for this
mesh path at all; the default is the vertex pulling of #NV25VM.

* Vertex shaders pull the same sites where mesh shaders will not run
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: NV25VM
:END:

RADV on this project's RX 6600 XT advertises =VK_EXT_mesh_shader= and accepts
the pipelines of #VAABY9, but does not reliably run them; lavapipe draws them
correctly and slowly, which is how the flat style was first seen working
here.  Rather than chase a young extension driver by driver, the atelier
gained a second /technique/ that needs nothing beyond Vulkan 1.0 and changes
nothing about the representation.
A renderer is made with =:technique :vertex= (the default, and
[[lisp:luft.render:*default-technique*]]) or =:technique :mesh=; the frame
block, the site and cell buffers, the bind group, every fragment stage, the
sky, and the lens are the same objects either way.  A technique is only
which modules are created, how a pipeline is described, and how a pass is
told to draw, and those three are EQL methods on the technique's name
([[lisp:luft.render::create-technique-pipelines]],
[[lisp:luft.render::draw-surface]], [[lisp:luft.render::draw-screen]]).

The vertex path draws $K$ vertices per site with no vertex buffer at all.
Each vertex shader invocation divides its built-in index by $K$ to find its
site in the very same =(unsigned-byte 64)= buffer, takes the remainder to
learn which corner of which triangle it is, and unpacks anchor, extent, and
polarity with the same =ldb= forms as the mesh lane (#AGVXGM).  A flat face
is six vertices, the two triangles $(0\,1\,2)(0\,2\,3)$ around the anchor
corner, reversed when the polarity is negative so the loop stays
counter-clockwise from outside.  A vertex cannot be skipped the way a mesh
lane's output count is uniform, so an absent site or a back-facing face
collapses every one of its vertices onto the anchor, and the rasterizer drops
the degenerate triangle.  The brick spheres of #VAABY9 are still computed
and uploaded but nothing culls by them; with no culling state in the
pipeline and twelve thousand faces in the demonstration world, clipping is
cheap enough that the first version simply draws everything.

The chamfer of #RUAWR5 is the same grid at fifty-four vertices a face: nine
quads of the $4	imes4$ point lattice, each split along the diagonal toward
its nearest corner as the mesh shader splits them.  Where one mesh lane
gathered all sixteen star cells and placed all sixteen points, a vertex is
one point and needs only the star of its own nearest corner: six cells
beside the face's solid and air cells, across $U$, across $V$, and across
both, toward that corner's side.  The site rules of #6TEFOS then move it --
corner star at a corner, edge star along a boundary, nowhere inside -- and
because every face incident to a site gathers the same star, the facets meet
exactly.  The watertightness test that was Metal-only now runs on Linux over
every style the default technique draws; the rounded =:bevel= style remains
mesh-only until its ring rule is written the same way.  The vertex shaders
live in =luft/render/vertex-shaders.lisp=.

* Fillets and bevels belong to edge and vertex sites
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 6TEFOS
:END:

Rounding a crease is not something either face beside it can decide alone,
and in LUFT it does not have to be: the crease is an edge site, its four
surrounding cells are its star, and a vertex site has eight.  The star's
solidity classifies the crease — flat, convex, concave, or saddle — and one
rule places the shared point: move toward the star's /minority/ (the lone
solid cell of a convex crease, the lone air cell of a concave one) so that
the point lands on the sphere or cylinder of radius $r$ centred at
$\text{site} + r\,q$, where $q$ is the minority direction clamped to unit
components.  For edges that is the fillet cylinder point at $r(1-1/\sqrt2)$,
for corners the sphere point at $r(1-1/\sqrt3)$, zero on flat and saddle
stars, and a vertex in the middle of a crease run agrees with its edge, so
creases stay straight.

Because every face incident to a site computes that point from the same star,
subdivided faces meet exactly and the surface is watertight without stitching
or corner patches.  =luft/render/shaders.lisp= subdivides each face 6×6:
boundary points use the site rule, ring points inside the radius project onto
the nearest crease's cylinder or the corner's sphere (a pure one-cell corner
is the exact rounded box), and mixed corners such as a block standing on a
floor blend toward the vertex sphere by distance.  The only CPU help is
[[lisp:luft:chain-cell-bits]], the solid chain as one bit per cell, from
which each mesh lane gathers its sixteen star cells; the rounding radius is a
live frame lane.  The exact morphological answer for the mixed stars would be
a table over the 256 vertex configurations, which #WQ611A's wave-level work
does not need and which this heuristic postpones.

* Small planar facets soften convex and concave creases
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: RUAWR5
:END:

The =:chamfer= style is the one-ring, planar case of #6TEFOS.  It applies the
same symmetric star classification as the rounded style: a convex crease
moves toward its minority solid cells, a concave crease moves toward its
minority air cells, and flat or saddle stars do not move.  Thus both exposed
silhouettes and inside meetings lose the perfectly sharp edge that makes a
block world look synthetic, without pretending the geometry is literal
woodworking.

A shared point moves by exactly $\tfrac{w}{2}\,q$, half the setback along its
clamped minority direction.  At an edge this is the midline of a flat
45-degree facet between two offset faces; at a pure corner it is where three
small chamfer planes meet.  The default =*chamfer-width*= is 0.045 cell:
enough to catch the studio lights and soften terrain notches without bringing
back the old 0.22-cell carved coves.  The rounded style keeps its independent
=*bevel-radius*=.

Shading comes from the geometry rather than interpolated vertex normals:
=chamfer-fragment-shader= reads each facet's normal from screen-space
derivatives of world position.  Only the arris where the original face meets
the cut receives a narrow =*arris-softness*= transition, currently 0.004 cell;
the chamfer itself stays planar.  The overhead view regression now exercises
rounded, flat, and chamfered pipelines and finds no sky-visible cracks.

** IDEA Compact surviving faces inside a brick
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: WQ611A
:END:

/Intent./ Today a culled or back-facing lane still costs four vertices and
two degenerate triangles because output counts must be uniform and the
language has no workgroup memory, atomics, or subgroup ballots.  A ballot
and prefix sum in the task stage would let a brick emit only its visible
faces, and a mesh workgroup could serve several sparse bricks.

/Evidence./ The current world draws thousands of faces at trivial cost, so
this is not yet a bottleneck; it is the natural next language extension
after #HFX2LI, and the first that touches wave-level semantics on both
Metal and SPIR-V.

/Done when./ A dense brick facing away from the camera emits zero mesh work,
measured on Metal, without changing the surface encoding of #AGVXGM.

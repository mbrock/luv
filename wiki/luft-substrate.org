#+title: Luft meets the luvcraft substrate
#+startup: overview

Luvcraft grew a semantic substrate: dimensional quantities checked through
one arithmetic medium with CPU and GPU realizations, columnar storage bound
to finite domains, and frontier programs compiled from declarations.  Luft
grew a topological substrate: packed sites, normalized chains, integer
manifold-sheet meshes, and a sparse colored light fixpoint.  The two were
deliberately developed apart, and that separation did its job — neither
paradigm distorted the other while it was being discovered.  This page reads
each side against the other and begins asking what Luft should adopt, in
which order, and with what evidence.

* Two substrates grew toward each other
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 3LS8TE
:END:

The luvcraft pages record a progression: quantities and units as inspectable
semantics erased before hot loops (#9P358P, #FTQEQD), one arithmetic language
with the shader compiler as merely its first client (#5N6SOQ), columnar
layout separated from materialization and policy (#Y0TPND), fixed
materializations bound to finite domains (#LC5ULQ), and finally frontier
programs whose transfer laws are quantity-checked arithmetic compiled into
closed scalar loops (#53Q1II, #T2G95K).

Luft's pages record a different progression: fixnum sites owning topology
(#KE4P5F), surfaces as boundaries of 3-chains (#9HLYEE), storage buffers and
bit fields entering the shader language so the GPU reads packed sites
directly (#AGVXGM, #HFX2LI), and an RGB4 light field solved by a packed
brightest-first frontier (#MMH4S2 uses it for spells).

The claim of this page is that these are not two paradigms but one paradigm
discovered twice at different altitudes.  Luvcraft found the /names/ —
domain, materialization, field, layout, program, family — and built checked
machinery for them.  Luft found the /instances/ — harder, more exact ones
than luvcraft ever had — while naming almost none of them.  Adoption
therefore does not mean importing a foreign framework; it means recognizing
convergent structure and deciding where the named, checked form pays for
itself.

* The substrate in four layers, and where each lives
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: IPA7ON
:END:

What "the luvcraft systems" actually are, by ownership:

1. /Quantity algebra and the arithmetic language/ — =luv/arithmetic=,
   =luv/arithmetic/language=, =luv/arithmetic/lisp=.  Dimensions, semantic
   kinds, units with admissibility, point/absolute/difference character,
   heterogeneous quantity layouts, semantic maps; checked definitions
   lowered to ordinary Lisp functions and to SPIR-V/MSL.  Backend-neutral
   already; nothing luvcraft-specific remains in it.
2. /Columnar records/ — =luv.arithmetic.records=
   ([[file:../arithmetic/records/columnar.lisp][=arithmetic/records/columnar.lisp=]]).  =define-columnar-buffer= for
   dynamic ordinal domains and =define-columnar-materialization= for fixed
   finite domains, both retaining quantity-aware row declarations while
   generating specialized lanes and raw borrow macros (#327W2B, #LDP5UR).
   Also neutral.
3. /Finite-domain vocabulary/ — =luv.domains=.  Small: cardinality plus
   identity/keyword vocabulary domains.  Neutral.
4. /The frontier language/ — =luvcraft.frontier=.  Program definitions,
   families (#FE0O5R), bindings (#716UN6), and the compiler to closed
   scalar loops.  This layer is /not/ neutral: its package uses
   =luvcraft.world=, and its neighborhood and window machinery are
   luvcraft's chunk vocabulary.  Its semantic surface is portable; its
   execution plans are not yet.

The ownership map matters because Luft's core system deliberately depends
only on =sb-simd=, while =luft/renderer= already depends on =luv=.  Layers 1–3
are pure Common Lisp and could enter Luft's core as dependencies; layer 4
would first need either extraction or a second realization.

* Luft already speaks the vocabulary without the names
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: SZC1FC
:END:

Reading Luft's code with luvcraft's glossary open is striking.

- [[lisp:luft:make-world-domain]] with [[lisp:luft:make-site]] and
  [[lisp:luft:checked-site]] is a finite domain in exactly the #B8R3KF sense:
  cardinality, an index type, checked index/offset conversion — the site
  packing /is/ the offset map, chosen so numeric order is chunk-contiguous.
  The =OUTSIDE-DOMAIN= and =MISSING-CHUNK= conditions with their
  =TREAT-AS-AIR= / =TREAT-AS-SOLID= / =USE-CHUNK= restarts are #H2W9DJ's
  boundary-environment separation, realized through the condition system:
  the domain owns local meaning, the caller's handler owns what lies beyond.
- [[lisp:luft:solve-voxel-light]] is a hand-written frontier program of the
  =:monotone-max-fixpoint= family: sixteen brightest-first buckets, admission
  only on a join that strictly improves, stale-pop detection, and retained
  =visits= / =pushes= / =stale-pops= counters that mirror luvcraft's frontier
  execution evidence almost line for line.  Compare its =admit= /
  =drain-neighbor= locals with the emitted drain of #PJY6E1.
- The mesher's instance, template, and light streams are hand-rolled
  columnar buffers: parallel adjustable =(unsigned-byte 32)= vectors with
  shared logical length, appended in lockstep, borrowed raw by the GPU
  upload.  That is #Y0TPND's storage contract minus the checked row meaning
  and minus the generated growth/retention discipline.
- The render population already uses =luv.domains= identity vocabularies:
  =*surface-assembly-vocabulary*= supplies members, offsets, and a revision
  that the population snapshots at its ownership boundary.  The stocks are
  a vocabulary domain in production today — the palette-domain
  materialization of #1RF48O is one step away.
- [[lisp:luft.render::pack-torch-flame-frame]] and its validator are a
  hand-written represented-value product: fixed lanes, finite-float checks,
  pack/unpack pairs — what a declared quantity product (#F8U2LA) generates
  and checks mechanically.

None of this is coincidence.  Both codebases were pushed by the same
pressures — exactness, inspectability, allocation-free hot loops — toward
the same shapes.  Luft's versions are leaner and more exact; luvcraft's are
named, checked, and reusable.

* The differences are design input, not friction
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: ASGG7Q
:END:

Four places where Luft is not a fourth client waiting to be plugged in, but
evidence that the substrate itself must grow.

/RGB4 light is a lattice, not a scalar./  Luvcraft's monotone family relaxes
one scalar field; its join is =max= and its priority is the value.  Luft's
join is [[lisp:luft:voxel-light-componentwise-max]] — a product-lattice join —
and its priority is a projection, the brightest lane.  The solver's own
comment records the consequence: an RGB join can retain a bright lane while
improving a dim one, so a site can be re-admitted /brighter/ than the bucket
currently draining, and =current-priority= must be dynamic rather than a
one-way scan.  That is a family law, not an implementation detail.  Adopting
the frontier language here means generalizing =:monotone-max-fixpoint= to a
declared join operation plus a priority projection, with Luft's dynamic
bucket behavior derived from the declaration.  The existing solver becomes
the differential oracle, exactly as luvcraft's legacy solver did.

/Execution plans differ; the semantic layer transfers./  Luft's hot
representations are Z-fiber occupancy words and sparse 8³ light pages, not
luvcraft's chunk windows.  #53Q1II already argues that layout is one part of
an execution plan and that the compiler may select different physical
realizations for the same program.  Luft is the first out-of-luvcraft test
of that claim — the test of whether the frontier language deserves a
neutral home or was secretly luvcraft's private idiom all along.

/The dependency posture is a real decision./  Quantity work in shaders costs
Luft nothing: =luft/renderer= already depends on =luv=.  Field definitions on
the light field and columnar materializations in the mesher would add
=luv/arithmetic= and =luv/domains= to Luft's core — pure-CL, slim-profile
compatible, but a deliberate departure from "depends on sb-simd only" that
should be made consciously, not absorbed.

/The mesher is not a frontier program./  It is a deterministic sweep: derive
chunk facts, radix-cancel sheet edges, emit sorted streams.  Forcing it into
frontier vocabulary would be the callback-protocol mistake #DURBKN warns
against.  Where frontier shapes may genuinely appear later is incremental
mesh invalidation and any gameplay reachability/aftermath propagation — the
#ZEENY3 dynamics, not the emission sweep.

* The shader lanes are an unlabeled ledger again
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: T5IRYG
:END:

Luvcraft's #P6DF7B recorded the motivating condition for quantity layouts: a
frame uniform whose vec4 lanes carried direction, proportion, radiance,
exposure, and biases distinguished only by comments.  Luft has rebuilt that
condition at larger scale.  Its scene uniform is twenty-seven anonymous
=vec4= members — =render-parameters=, =temporal-parameters=,
=character-parameters=, =fireball-parameters=, and their previous-frame
twins — and the member list is repeated textually /fourteen times/ across
the shader definitions of =luft/render/shaders.lisp= and
=luft/render/flame-shaders.lisp=, with no shared declaration site at all.  Meanwhile the fragment ladder runs genuine radiometry: HDR
radiance, Rec. 709 luminance, log-luminance exposure metering, tone mapping,
paper grading — every lane of #T1GYOH's table, every one a bare float.

This is the adoption with the highest immediate value and the lowest risk,
because luvcraft has already proven the migration property that matters:
declaring quantities on shader interfaces and uniform members changes no
generated code.  Byte-identical SPIR-V was the acceptance test through the
whole luvcraft migration, and it found real bugs anyway — the sky-colour
character mismatch of #N6S2CX, the clip-origin confusion of #9ZK7Q7.

The spell-effects work makes this concrete rather than hygienic.  The effect
frame of #TM41II is a new packed product about to be born; the exposure
policies of #POPM2I are decisions /about/ specific HDR lanes; and #K8MOXW
asks the effect frame to carry a composition class.  A frame that is born as
a declared quantity product — HDR radiance lanes named as radiance, ages and
lifetimes as durations, seeds as categorical words, spine endpoints as
world-position points with their previous-frame twins — gets its
=validate-torch-flame-frame= for free and gives the CPU writer and shader
reader one checked contract, which is precisely #TZHN8G's promise that both
sides of the bridge are Lisp.

* Light and stocks are fields over finite domains
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: GSPSG4
:END:

Two readings of existing Luft code through #FKAV5Y's identity /
meaning / representation split.

The voxel light field: its identity is "local colored light"; its value
meaning is RGB4, three 0..15 non-negative absolutes packed in twelve bits;
its representations are already plural — sparse u16 pages on the CPU, a
u32-packed two-word sidecar per instance in the render population, a
=uvec2= storage buffer decoded with =ldb= in the vertex shader, normalized
floats after decode.  Nothing ties these four representations to one
declared meaning today; a field definition would.  The open semantic
question #WEE8P5 raised for luvcraft — is a light level an encoding of a
proportion, or a meaningful scaled unit with "lose one per step" as its
law? — lands harder in Luft, where the mesh-point sampler interpolates in
level space and rounds.  The first field experiment should force that
decision and record it.

The material side: =solve-voxel-light= currently takes a hash table from
cell sites to material indices plus an opacity vector — a row-shaped probe
inside the hottest loop, the #J7A2KD smell.  The named shape exists and is
half-adopted already: =*surface-assembly-vocabulary*= is an identity
vocabulary domain, and #1RF48O's palette-domain materialization — u8
opacity and emission lanes over the vocabulary, CLOS dispatch once per
member, dense lookup in the loop — is exactly what the solver's
=opacity-table= wants to grow into, with the difference that Luft's would
be checked against the vocabulary's revision.  Occupancy and material
should eventually be sibling materializations over the same cell domain
(#A6X2RT, #HAUW7L), which is also what a future absorbing-media
integration (#UYECJA) will want to sample.

* What compiled frontier programs would buy Luft
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: CRO1IF
:END:

The near-term candidates, in order of evidence strength:

1. /Voxel light addition./  The program declaration writes itself — fields
   =(light :relaxed :join componentwise-max :priority brightest-lane)= and
   =opacity=, transfer as attenuation through the checked palette lanes —
   and the current solver is a ready-made oracle with retained counters for
   exact A/B comparison.  The generalized family of #ASGG7Q is the price;
   the reward is that spell aftermath (#MMH4S2's persistent handoff)
   arrives as =admit= / =relate= entries on a realization instead of ad hoc
   solver variants.
2. /Incremental relight./  Luft currently re-solves from scratch per scene.
   The moment gameplay edits light-bearing cells at interactive rates —
   burning cells, placed crystals, the #YRDFFH aftermath — Luft needs
   exactly the =:invalidation= family with a companion frontier (#ZEENY3),
   and writing it by hand twice would be the signal that the language
   should have been shared.
3. /Discovery clients./  Room detection for the indoors work (#75OOSW
   option three) is a textbook =:discover-once= program over cell
   adjacency with a roofed-predicate admission — the same shape as
   terminal walls (#55IRJO).

The evidence standard carries over unchanged: exact field and visit
equality against the retained oracle, allocation ceilings, Tracy zones at
the same semantic boundaries.  Luft's determinism culture makes this easier,
not harder.

* What this page does not decide
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: RI26G7
:END:

- Whether the frontier language moves to a neutral =luv/frontiers= home with
  domain and neighborhood protocols, or Luft grows a sibling realization
  and the extraction happens on the third client.  The smallest honest
  extraction is not yet scoped.
- Whether Luft's core accepts =luv/arithmetic= and =luv/domains= as
  dependencies, or the field and columnar adoptions stay renderer-side
  until the core earns them.
- Whether RGB4 light is one lattice-valued field or three scalar fields
  under one product join — the solver's dynamic-priority behavior argues
  for the lattice reading, but the family design should decide with both
  candidates written down.
- The level-versus-proportion semantics of a light step (#WEE8P5's open
  question, Luft edition).
- Whether mesh instance streams migrate to =define-columnar-buffer= or the
  substrate first grows the compaction vocabulary #34G2K8 already names —
  the mesher's streams never remove rows, so it may be the easier client.

* NEXT Declare the Luft frame and effect-frame lanes
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: S1IABD
:END:

/Intent./  Adopt the quantity paradigm where it costs no dependency and no
codegen: give Luft's scene uniform one shared, quantity-declared member
list, declare the torch-flame frame (and the spell-effect frame of #TM41II
when it lands) as checked represented-value products, and name the HDR
radiance, luminance, and exposure lanes through the presentation ladder.
Categorical words — seeds, template ids, packed site words — stay
deliberately unannotated per #J4V8QM.

/Evidence./  The member list is repeated fourteen times today with no
shared owner; luvcraft's migration (#9P358P, #S00PRF, #9ZK7Q7) proved
byte-identical SPIR-V throughout while surfacing real character and origin
mistakes; =validate-torch-flame-frame= hand-implements what declared
products generate; and the spell-effects experiment is about to multiply
exactly these lanes.

/Done when./  One declaration site owns the scene uniform's members with
their quantities and layouts; every Luft shader splices it; the torch-flame
frame is a declared product whose validator is derived rather than
hand-written; at least the radiance→luminance→exposure→tonemap chain
carries named quantities through checked arithmetic; all production Luft
SPIR-V modules are byte-identical before and after (any intentional
divergence is its own recorded change); and the migration's list of
surprised lanes — mislabeled, mischaracterized, or genuinely ambiguous —
is recorded on this page as the analogue of luvcraft's audit findings.

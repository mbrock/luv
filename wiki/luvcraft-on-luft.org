#+title: Luvcraft on a Luft core
#+startup: overview

An inventory, taken in October 2026, of what it would mean to move the
Luvcraft game onto Luft, or, read the other way, to grow in Luft what
Luvcraft has and Luft lacks.  It extends the crossing rule of #WO50UR, under
which a feature moves once Luft owns its command and state transition and its
Luvcraft form is then deleted, with a map of every feature and the seams
that will be hard.  It deliberately does not propose a common engine; the
workbench pages (#QRP6YG, #Z96DF9) explain why.

* The two games are closer than their code suggests
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: BBKLUG
:END:

Luvcraft is about 54,000 lines: a world model, a CPU mesher, a frontier
light solver, streaming, a 3,200-line renderer, physics, critters, agents,
and a long tail of devices in the world -- terminals, a phone, portals,
video screens, tapes, fireworks.  Luft is about 28,000: a topology core, a
star-atlas renderer whose plain cubes now cost less than Luvcraft's and whose
bevels about one and a half to two times as much (#8TUEPF, #72Y245, #A4ZWDZ), streaming, a walking character, editing, and a McCLIM
atelier on the shared workbench.  Luft has almost none of the game; Luvcraft
has an older version of nearly all of the machinery.

The surprise is the world itself.  Luvcraft's 72 block kinds are every one an
opaque full cube -- =blocks.lisp= says so: "the world has no transparent
cells" -- and a Luft cell is exactly one occupancy bit and one material from
a vocabulary of up to 255.  Every Luvcraft world is already a Luft world;
=scripts/terrain-comparison.lisp= converts one cell for cell (#PWX5RT).  What
differs is what a material means and what lives beside the cells.

* What maps directly, and what does not
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: R15JJS
:END:

| Concern | Luvcraft | Luft | Crossing |
|-
| Cell | 72 opaque cube kinds | solid bit + material, ≤255 | direct |
| Axes, chunk | Y up; 16×64×16 | Z up; 64×64×255 columns | coordinate map only |
| Material appearance | painted 16-px texture and normal tiles per face | top, side, and bottom tones | *decision* |
| Block behaviour | tape, film, fountain, lava spring, terminal kinds | none | needs a behaviour layer |
| Light | sky and block light, incremental, streamed | RGB4 field, off in the streamed world | port or finish |
| Generator | meadow and alpine relief, caves, crystals, trees, rocks | alpine relief already ported | rest is pure functions |
| Edits and saves | seed + sparse edit overlay + resume + inhabitants | seed + sparse edits + pose + hour | same shape; extend |

Luvcraft's generator is the easiest substantial thing to move: its noise and
height functions are pure and only its materialization writes chunks, and
Luft's authored world already took the alpine relief that way.  Saves have
the same form on both sides, a seed and a sparse overlay, so an old
Luvcraft save could be replayed onto a Luft world built from the ported
generator.

About a quarter of Luvcraft, some 13,000 lines of renderer, meshing,
streaming, walking, sky, world text, and terminal walls, is machinery Luft
has already rebuilt and would simply delete.  Roughly 12--15k lines are
neutral and move nearly as they are.  The rest sits on five seams.

* Five seams carry the cost
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: NFNM1C
:END:

1. *Appearance.*  Luvcraft paints a texture and a normal map per face;
   Luft's star shading has tones and bevels, and no textures.  Either Luft's
   materials gain an atlas that the star patches sample by face, or the 72
   kinds become tones and Luvcraft's look changes.  Everything downstream --
   the inventory icons, the gazetteer's views, the way a child recognizes a
   block -- follows this choice.

2. *Light.*  Luvcraft's sky and block light is incremental, streamed, and
   paid for by a compiled frontier language; Luft's RGB4 field is solid but
   not streamed.  #LX8GZ2 already names Luft needing streamed light as the
   trigger to move the frontier language below both games.

3. *The overlay and device seam.*  About twenty Luvcraft files hang off one
   session protocol whose stages -- scene, world panel, viewmodel, held
   display, HUD -- are Luvcraft's own passes.  Phones, portals, video
   screens, held items, and the hands all need equivalents in Luft's frame
   graph.  This is the largest piece of work that is neither deletion nor a
   straight port.  The workbench notes (#M9MI6Y) say Luvcraft's overlays are
   not the substrate's template, so Luft should grow its own stages, one
   device at a time.

4. *Block behaviour.*  Tapes, films, fountains, lava springs, and terminal
   blocks are kinds with behaviour; a Luft cell has only a material.  A small
   behaviour table keyed by material, with persistent per-cell state where a
   kind needs it, would carry them.

5. *Agents' words.*  The gnome's tools and prompts speak integer cells,
   "y is up", and block boxes.  The plumbing moves unchanged; the tool
   vocabulary is rewritten in Luft's terms, Z up and material names.

* An order for the crossings
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: Q758FV
:END:

Each step leaves both games working, and each deletes its Luvcraft side
once it has crossed.  Steps that only need a decision come first; the
overlay seam comes once a device needs it.

** TODO Decide how Luft materials look: tones, or textures by face
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: Q3KI72
:END:

/Intent./ Choose whether the 72 Luvcraft kinds cross as Luft tones or
whether Luft's star patches sample a face texture atlas.  Measure the cost
of sampling a texture in the star fragment stage on the exported world
(#PWX5RT) before deciding.

/Done when./ The exported world renders with every Luvcraft kind
recognizably itself, in whichever form, at a measured cost.

** TODO Port the rest of Luvcraft's generator as Luft world sources
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: MF8JGD
:END:

/Intent./ Move caves, crystals, trees, and rocks into Luft's authored world
the way the alpine relief crossed, so a seed raises the same world in both
games.  Then replay a Luvcraft save onto it.

/Done when./ One Luvcraft save opens in Luft with its edits in place.

** TODO Stream voxel light in Luft's large world
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: BQMYNU
:END:

/Intent./ Turn #JDB26Y back on: regional sky and block light in the streamed
world, extracting the frontier language under #LX8GZ2 if Luft's own solver
cannot be streamed cheaply.

/Done when./ Torches and lanterns light the streamed world, and Luvcraft's
light solver has a replacement it can be deleted for.

** TODO Carry a block, and fill an inventory
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: 291ELY
:END:

/Intent./ Give the Luft player a carried material and a quickbar, reusing
the neutral McCLIM inventory and hotbar, and make placement draw from it.

/Done when./ Place, mine, and pick in Luft follow what the player carries,
and Luvcraft's inventory code serves both or is deleted.

** IDEA Grow Luft's own device stages one device at a time
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: CS2DOF
:END:

/Intent./ Begin the overlay seam with the cheapest real device -- held items
and first-person hands, then the phone -- as stages of Luft's frame, not as a
port of Luvcraft's session protocol.  Physics bodies (with a fiber terrain
probe), critters, and agents with bodies follow on the same stages.

/Done when./ One held device works in Luft and its Luvcraft host is gone.
